The SportsMax App — Cord-Cutter Option

If you do not have a cable subscription, or your local provider does not offer a sports package, the SportsMax App is the most practical solution for Caribbean fans. It is a standalone streaming service available across the region with no cable contract required.

The SportsMax App gives you live and on-demand access to all three SportsMax channels — covering all 104 matches of the 2026 FIFA World Cup. It works on iOS, Android, web browsers, and Smart TVs, so you can watch on your phone, tablet, laptop, or television.

Kickoff Times for Caribbean Fans

The 2026 World Cup is hosted in North America — the USA, Canada, and Mexico — which means for the first time in a generation, Caribbean fans are watching a tournament playing in a nearby time zone. Most matches fall within the Atlantic Standard Time (AST) and Eastern Standard Time (EST) window, making for genuinely watchable kickoffs.

The majority of the Caribbean sits in UTC-4 (AST/ECT), though Jamaica and Belize observe UTC-5 (EST/CST). Puerto Rico and the US Virgin Islands are UTC-4 year-round.

Good news for Caribbean fans: Unlike the 2022 World Cup in Qatar (late nights) or any European tournament (pre-dawn kickoffs), the 2026 edition plays almost entirely in the afternoon and evening for Caribbean viewers. For Jamaican fans, kickoff times are even friendlier — the latest evening fixtures from the West Coast finish before 10pm local time. Group stage matches are concentrated in the early-to-mid afternoon AST window, ideal for watching after work or school.

Caribbean Teams at the 2026 World Cup

The expanded 48-team format has delivered a historic moment for the Caribbean — two Caribbean nations are at the 2026 FIFA World Cup. Haiti qualified through CONCACAF and Curaçao through UEFA, giving the region representation across two entirely different footballing paths. All of their matches air on SportsMax across the Caribbean.

Curaçao — Group E

Curaçao are in Group E alongside Germany, Ecuador, and Ivory Coast. Curaçao qualified through UEFA — a remarkable achievement for the Dutch Caribbean island, who compete in the European qualifying rounds due to their football association's historical affiliation. This is Curaçao's first-ever FIFA World Cup appearance.

Watching Free with a VPN

There is no free-to-air option for the 2026 World Cup in the Caribbean — but if you have a VPN, you can access completely free streams from other countries' public broadcasters. Several major markets air all 104 matches at no cost, and their streaming services are simply geo-restricted to their home territory.
