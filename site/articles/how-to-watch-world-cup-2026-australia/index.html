Who Has the Rights in Australia

Australian football fans have landed the best deal of any major market: SBS holds exclusive broadcast rights to the 2026 FIFA World Cup, and every single one of the 104 matches will air live and completely free. No subscription. No pay wall. No splitting games across platforms.

SBS has been Australia's home of World Cup football since 1986 — that's 40 consecutive years of free-to-air coverage, a run that's now comfortably longer than most Socceroos fans have been alive. The 2022 edition from Qatar, supercharged by a Socceroos run to the Round of 16, drove over 80 million hours of content viewed on SBS platforms. The 2026 deal extends that legacy with an even bigger tournament.

Important clarification: Optus Sport held the 2022 World Cup rights alongside SBS. For 2026, SBS is the only official broadcaster. Do not pay for Optus Sport expecting World Cup coverage — it has no rights to this tournament.

SBS: The Complete Breakdown

All 104 matches are spread across three SBS platforms — the main channel, SBS VICELAND, and SBS On Demand. The split allows SBS to run simultaneous matches without either fixture losing coverage.

Watch on SBS On Demand

The Socceroos' group stage matches will all be broadcast on the main SBS channel with commentary from David Basheer and former Socceroo Luke Wilkshire. SBS's punditry lineup includes Harry Kewell, Lydia Williams, Tommy Oar, Andrew Redmayne, Kevin-Prince Boateng, and Sarah Walsh — the strongest studio team the broadcaster has assembled for a tournament.

"SBS will deliver more than 550 hours of FIFA World Cup programming — live matches, studio coverage, mini-matches, highlights, and archival content."

Beyond match coverage, SBS On Demand will carry a dedicated Socceroos vodcast throughout the tournament, plus classic World Cup matches via the FIFA+ FAST channel integrated into the platform.

Pro tip: Update your SBS On Demand app before the tournament starts to make sure you have the latest version. Enable notifications to get automatic alerts for Socceroos matches and breaking team news.

Kickoff Times for Australian Fans

The 2026 World Cup is hosted across the United States, Canada, and Mexico — all operating in North American time zones. For Australian viewers on AEST (UTC+10), this creates a genuinely favourable schedule compared to most previous tournaments.

SBS has confirmed that 69% of all live matches kick off between 6:00am and 4:30pm AEST — meaning most games land at manageable morning and early afternoon viewing times. That's a significant shift from European-hosted tournaments, which routinely pushed into overnight viewing for Australian fans.

Socceroos Group D Schedule

Australia are in Group D alongside co-hosts the United States, Paraguay, and Türkiye. It's Australia's seventh World Cup appearance overall and sixth in a row — a run that stretches back to Germany 2006. Head coach Tony Popovic guided the Socceroos through direct AFC qualification, finishing second behind Japan in Group C with 19 points from 10 matches.

All three Socceroos group stage matches take place on the US West Coast — Vancouver, Seattle, and Santa Clara. Good news for the schedule: two of the three games land at afternoon and lunchtime AEST, with only the USA fixture requiring an early alarm.

If you can't stay up, the full replay is available on SBS On Demand from shortly after the final whistle.

If the Socceroos advance

The top two teams from each group, plus the eight best third-placed teams, advance to the Round of 32. If Australia progress, their Round of 32 fixture would fall between July 1–8. All knockout matches through to the final air live and free on SBS. Specific dates and venues depend on group stage results and bracket position.
