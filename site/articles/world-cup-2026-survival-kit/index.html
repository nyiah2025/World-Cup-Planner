Why 39 Items for 39 Days

The 2026 World Cup runs from 11 June to 19 July — 39 days, 104 matches, three host nations, and more football than any tournament in history. That is a lot of football to sit through, and doing it properly requires more than a sofa and goodwill. It requires preparation.

This list is built for the fan who intends to take the tournament seriously: every group game tracked, every knockout match watched, the wall chart on the wall from day one. Some items are essential. Some are upgrades. All 39 are genuinely useful across the full 39-day run.

We've organised everything into sections — big screen, streaming, audio, shirts, snacks, comfort — so you can work through it category by category. Affiliate links where relevant; skip them if you already own it. And if you're sorting out your tech setup first, our full streaming devices guide goes deeper on the device side of things.

The Big Screen

Everything else on this list is a supporting act. The main event is the screen. Get this right first.

Items 1–4 · The Big Screen
#01
⭐ The Centrepiece
65"+ 4K TV
This is the decision that changes everything. A 65-inch screen is the minimum for a genuine group-watching experience — as the viewing party guide explains, the difference between a 43-inch and a 65-inch TV in a room of six people is night and day. The Hisense U8 Series hits the sweet spot: Mini-LED panel, Dolby Vision, 144Hz, and VIDAA's broad streaming app support, at a price point that makes the upgrade genuinely justifiable for a 39-day tournament. Go 75 inches if your room allows it — you won't regret it. Position the screen at seated eye level, not mounted high on a wall where everyone cranes their necks for ninety minutes.

#02
🔊 Atmosphere
Soundbar or Bluetooth Speaker
TV speakers are one of the great lies of the consumer electronics industry. A screen that costs a thousand pounds ships with audio that sounds like it came from a tin can. A soundbar — even a modest one — transforms the atmosphere completely. The crowd roar should fill the room, not leak out of it. For groups of four or more, the Sony HT-S400 or Samsung HW-550F hit the right price-to-impact ratio. If you're setting up in a second room or garden, a good Bluetooth speaker — the JBL Charge or Bose SoundLink Flex — covers it cleanly.

#03
📺 Picture Quality
HDMI 2.1 Cable
If you have a 4K HDR TV and a streaming device or console capable of 4K output, you need an HDMI 2.1 cable to carry the full signal without bottlenecking. The cable that came in the box with your TV is almost certainly not HDMI 2.1 — it's probably 2.0 or lower. A certified HDMI 2.1 cable costs very little and unlocks 4K/120Hz, HDR10+, and Dolby Vision passthrough. Buy two: one for the TV, one spare. The difference between a properly connected 4K signal and a throttled one is visible from the sofa.

#04
🔧 Setup
Full-Motion TV Wall Mount or Tilt Bracket
If your TV is mounted high on a wall — which most are — tilt it down. The centre of the screen should sit at roughly seated eye level. Watching a 90-minute match with your neck craned upward is a quick route to genuine discomfort, and with a 39-day tournament ahead, it's worth getting this right before the opening match. A tilt bracket is an easy weekend job. A full-motion articulating arm is better still — it lets you angle the screen perfectly for every seat configuration, which matters when you're hosting a group.

Multi-Port USB-C Charging Hub
During a big match with guests, the number of devices that need charging is always higher than the number of available sockets. A multi-port USB-C hub with at least four ports — including one high-wattage PD port for laptops — solves this cleanly. The Anker Prime Charging Station is the benchmark: multiple ports, high total output, and a design that doesn't look industrial on a living room shelf. #21
📶 Connectivity
Wi-Fi 6 Mesh Router or Extender
Live sport is one of the most demanding streaming scenarios — millions of people hitting the same CDN simultaneously the moment a goal goes in. A weak Wi-Fi signal in the TV room is the single most common cause of streaming failures during peak moments. If your router is in a different room from your TV, a Wi-Fi 6 mesh node or range extender pointed at the viewing room makes a meaningful difference. The TP-Link Deco XE75 is a solid mesh system. If you can run an Ethernet cable from the router directly to the TV or streaming device, always do that first — wired beats wireless every time for live sports.

🔌 Practical
Travel Extension Lead / Power Strip
A three-socket power strip with USB-A and USB-C ports is one of those purchases that immediately becomes indispensable. It lives behind the TV for the duration of the tournament: streaming device plugged in, soundbar plugged in, USB ports charging the tablet and the phone simultaneously. If you're watching in a garden, a summer room, or anywhere without multiple sockets nearby, a long-lead extension is a non-negotiable add. The Anker Power Strip with surge protection is the one to get — it also protects your equipment against power surges, which matters during a thunderstorm halfway through extra time.

Football Shirts

Wear the shirt. This is not optional. The World Cup is one of the few occasions in adult life where wearing a football shirt in a non-stadium context is completely socially acceptable. Take the opportunity.

Items 26–28 · Football Shirts
#26
⭐ Primary
Your Nation's Home Shirt
The home shirt. Order it now — not the week before the first match, now. Popular nations' shirts sell out in common sizes well before the tournament starts, and replica kit suppliers don't always restock quickly. The official kit is always worth the extra over the replica. The difference in quality is visible and the feeling of watching a tournament in an official shirt versus a market replica is, for reasons that are hard to fully explain, meaningfully different. Order one size up if you plan to wear it for 39 days straight over normal clothes.

Snacks & Drinks

The single worst thing you can do at a football match is serve a meal that requires cutlery. Finger food, grazing options, and a well-stocked drinks situation — these are the fundamentals of a functioning viewing setup for 39 days.

#29
🍿 Classic
Popcorn Machine or Microwave Popcorn Variety Pack
Popcorn is the most-efficient watching food in existence: one-handed, generates no plates, produces minimal mess, and can be made in four minutes. A microwave popcorn variety pack covers the group stage. A countertop popcorn machine — the kind with the crank handle and the hot plate — is the upgrade for knockout-round parties. It doubles as a conversation piece, a genuine improvement on the bag version, and it smells like a cinema, which is exactly the atmosphere you're trying to create.

#30
🌮 The Standard
Nachos Kit
Tortilla chips, salsa, guacamole, sour cream. The unofficial food of the modern football viewing experience. It's a North American-hosted World Cup, it's appropriate, and it scales infinitely — more guests, more chips. The key is separate bowls for the toppings rather than a pre-mixed pile. It's easier to serve, stays fresher, and gives everyone agency. Tip: warm the chips in the oven for four minutes before kickoff.
