Short Answer

Nigeria did not qualify for the 2026 FIFA World Cup after failing to progress through the CAF qualifiers. The Super Eagles will not be in the United States, Canada, or Mexico this summer. However, Nigerian fans still have strong broadcast options to follow all 104 matches of the tournament.

NTA, SuperSport & Showmax — Who Shows What

Nigeria's World Cup viewing landscape is built around three tiers: free-to-air terrestrial coverage from NTA, comprehensive satellite coverage via SuperSport on DStv, and streaming via Showmax Sports. MultiChoice, which operates DStv and Showmax across Sub-Saharan Africa, holds the broadest rights to the tournament in the region.

Which Matches Does NTA Show Free?

NTA's free-to-air coverage focuses on the highest-profile fixtures from the tournament — typically the opening match, semifinals, and the Final — as well as selected group stage matches involving prominent African teams and globally followed nations. The exact match list is confirmed closer to the tournament by NTA and FIFA, but Nigerian fans have traditionally been able to catch the biggest games without a subscription.

NTA has broadcast every FIFA World Cup since Nigeria's first appearance in 1994. Free-to-air coverage of the biggest matches is a long-standing tradition — even in years when the Super Eagles are absent.

For the full tournament — every group stage match, every knockout game, right through to the Final — you'll need SuperSport via DStv or Showmax Sports. NTA's selection, while free and accessible across the country, does not cover the complete 104-match schedule.

Streaming Without a Dish — Showmax & DStv Stream

For fans who want every match but don't have a DStv satellite setup, there are two strong streaming options that require nothing more than a smartphone, tablet, or Smart TV and a data connection.

Option 1 — Showmax Sports (most affordable)

Showmax Sports is the most cost-effective way to watch all 104 matches in Nigeria without a dish. The Showmax Sports plan gives access to live SuperSport channels and on-demand match replays. It works on iOS, Android, web browsers, and selected Smart TVs. At roughly ₦6,200/mo, subscribing for June and July covers the entire tournament for around ₦12,400 — a straightforward option for cord-cutters and mobile-first viewers.

Option 2 — DStv Stream

DStv Stream is MultiChoice's dish-free streaming service, giving access to DStv channels — including all SuperSport channels — via the DStv Stream app. It requires no satellite installation and works across mobile devices, tablets, and Smart TVs. Pricing is in line with standard DStv Compact packages. This is the better option if you also want access to DStv's broader entertainment channels beyond football.

Option 3 — DStv Satellite (existing subscribers)

If you already have a DStv satellite dish and a Compact or higher package, you're fully covered — all SuperSport channels carrying all 104 matches are already included. No additional subscriptions required.

Kickoff Times for Nigerian Fans (WAT)

Nigeria observes West Africa Time (WAT), which is UTC+1 year-round — no daylight saving adjustments. With the 2026 World Cup hosted across three North American time zones, most matches fall in the evening in Nigeria, making this one of the most viewer-friendly tournaments in decades for West African fans.

Great viewing hours: The majority of group stage matches kick off between 5pm and 11pm WAT — prime evening viewing. Only the West Coast late evening slots (6pm PT) push into the early hours. This is a far more fan-friendly schedule than tournaments held in Asia or Europe, where Nigerian viewers faced early morning or overnight kickoffs.

The most common kickoff window — matches starting at 2pm–3pm local (ET/CT) — lands squarely at 8pm WAT, ideal for evening viewing. The tournament's most high-profile fixtures, including the semifinals and Final, tend to kick off in this window.

Despite Nigeria's absence, African football has a strong presence at the 2026 World Cup. Nine CAF nations qualified for the tournament — the largest African contingent in World Cup history, reflecting the expansion from 32 to 48 teams. For Nigerian fans, these are the sides to follow.

Nigeria will be in full qualification mode for that tournament. The 2030 African qualifying campaign will begin in 2027.

Watching Nigerian Streams Abroad

DStv Stream, Showmax Sports, and NTA's online streams are all geo-restricted to Nigeria and other supported African territories. If you're a Nigerian fan travelling or living abroad during the tournament, you won't be able to access any of these services without a VPN.

A VPN (Virtual Private Network) lets you connect to a Nigerian or African server, allowing you to access DStv Stream and Showmax Sports from anywhere in the world. Alternatively, connecting to a UK server gives access to BBC iPlayer and ITVX — both free and streaming all 104 matches live. A US server gives access to Peacock and FS1 live streams.

Top-rated VPN options for streaming sports include NordVPN, ExpressVPN, and Surfshark. All are available from the How to Watch page. Note: using a VPN to access geo-restricted services may conflict with the provider's terms of service. Always check local laws before use.
