You don't need a match ticket to experience the 2026 World Cup. Across all 16 host cities — spread across the USA, Canada, and Mexico — FIFA has set up official Fan Festivals: free, open-air viewing hubs where fans can watch live matches on giant screens, catch concerts, try interactive experiences, eat, drink, and be surrounded by tens of thousands of people feeling exactly the same thing you are. It is, without question, the most accessible version of a World Cup ever staged.

Some of these festivals are epic in scale: Philadelphia runs for all 39 days of the tournament. Houston brings the games to a 250,000-square-foot entertainment district in EaDo. Monterrey has booked Imagine Dragons and Enrique Iglesias. Mexico City has turned the Zócalo — one of the largest plazas on earth — into the centrepiece of a tournament-long celebration. And Toronto kicked off the entire thing with a Grammy-produced countdown concert the night before a ball was kicked.

What follows is everything you need to know about every official FIFA Fan Fest location — venue, dates, entertainment, and the direct link to plan your visit. Whether you're in the host country or just flying in for the experience, this is your city-by-city guide.

All Fan Festivals are free and open to the public unless otherwise noted. Ticketed concerts at some venues are separate from the free general Fan Festival programming. Always check the official city site for capacity registration requirements — several venues require you to reserve a free entry pass in advance.
🇺🇸 United States — 11 Host Cities

🏙️
City 01 of 16
Atlanta, Georgia

📍 Venue Centennial Olympic Park, Atlanta, GA 30313
📅 Dates June 11 – July 15, 2026 (select match days)
🎤 Performers Davido Nappy Roots Kat Graham CeeLo Green
🎟️ Admission Free and open to the public
🔗 Official atlantafwc26.com/fan-fest

Centennial Olympic Park is one of the most loaded pieces of ground in American sporting history — the site of the 1996 Atlanta Olympics, the park where the flame burned. Now it's back at the centre of a global event, and it feels entirely right. Atlanta's Fan Fest has leaned into the city's musical identity in a way that most others haven't: Davido brings Afrobeats to the party, CeeLo Green is as Atlanta as it gets, and the Nappy Roots addition speaks to the city's deep hip-hop roots. Kat Graham — actress, singer, and daughter of an African father — rounds out a lineup that reflects exactly who Atlanta is in 2026.

Atlanta doesn't host group stage matches (its games are a little later in the schedule), which actually works in the Fan Fest's favour — the energy concentrates around the city's specific match dates rather than diffusing across a packed group-stage calendar. If you're in the South East and looking for the full outdoor World Cup experience, Centennial Park delivers it with serious production values and a performer lineup that no other US city comes close to on cultural range.

🏙️
City 02 of 16
Boston, Massachusetts

📍 Venue Boston City Hall Plaza, 1 City Hall Square, Boston, MA 02203
📅 Dates June 12 – June 27, 2026 (16 days)
🎤 Performers Cultural Showcase programme — full lineup TBC closer to the festival
🎟️ Admission Free and open to the public
🔗 Official bostonfwc26.com/fifa-fan-festival

Boston City Hall Plaza is one of those brutalist civic spaces that somehow works magnificently as a festival ground — all that open concrete becomes an asset once you fill it with giant screens, food vendors, and twenty thousand people celebrating something together. The city runs its Fan Fest across 16 days through the group stage window, which is the right call: Boston's matches cluster in the opening weeks and the festival is built around them.

Boston hasn't announced a headline concert lineup yet, which is the one gap compared to cities like Philly and Dallas. What it has confirmed is a Cultural Showcase series that will draw on the city's dense immigrant communities — one of the more interesting cultural propositions of any US Fan Fest. If you're a fan of good football, a walkable city, and a Fan Fest that doesn't feel like a product placement exercise, Boston in June is a genuinely compelling proposition. The city runs beautifully in summer. City Hall Plaza won't be its best-looking venue, but it will be alive.

🏙️
City 03 of 16
Dallas, Texas

📍 Venue Fair Park — Pavilion Amphitheater, Lots 9 & 10, Dallas, TX
📅 Dates June 11 – July 19, 2026 (all match days; capacity 35,000)
🎤 Performers Major Lazer Turnpike Troubadours Latin Legacy Tour MC Magic Baby Bash Lil Rob
🎟️ Admission Free general Fan Fest; ticketed concerts separate
🔗 Official dallasfwc26.com/our-venues/fan-festival

Dallas has done this properly. Fair Park is a magnificent setting — a 277-acre Art Deco complex that has hosted everything from the State Fair of Texas to Cotton Bowl classics, and it knows how to hold a crowd at scale. Thirty-five thousand fans, an amphitheater stage, and a concert series that reads like someone actually thought about who goes to a World Cup in 2026: Major Lazer for the global dance crowd, Turnpike Troubadours for Texas country fans, and a Latin Legacy Tour that puts MC Magic, Baby Bash, and Lil Rob together for what is, frankly, a great Chicano hip-hop night out.

Fair Park also benefits from running the full tournament — all match days, June 11 through July 19. Dallas is hosting group stage matches and potential knockout ties, which means the Fan Fest will be operating at full pitch for some of the biggest games of the tournament. If you're in North Texas for a match and want to extend the experience into the evening, Fair Park is where you go.

🏙️
City 04 of 16
Houston, Texas

📍 Venue East Downtown (EaDo), near Shell Energy Stadium — Polk to Walker, Emancipation to Hutchins
📅 Dates June 11 – July 19, 2026 (all 34 match days; soft-launched June 9)
🎤 Performers Baby Bash Frankie J Trae Tha Truth Mike Jones Coffey Anderson DJ Mei
📐 Scale 250,000 sq ft · up to 15,000 visitors daily
🎟️ Admission Free, all ages
🔗 Official fwc26houston.com/fanfestival

Houston's Fan Fest is planted in EaDo — one of those neighbourhoods that has spent the last decade quietly becoming the most interesting part of the city — and the 250,000-square-foot footprint it occupies is genuinely enormous. This is an entertainment district-scale activation, not a cordoned-off park. Fifteen thousand visitors a day for a month is a serious operation, and Houston has built it to handle that traffic.

The performer lineup is unapologetically Houston. Trae Tha Truth is the city's most beloved rap institution. Mike Jones (who? Mike Jones) remains a canonical figure. Baby Bash and Frankie J cover the Latin R&B lane that runs deep in this city's demographics. Coffey Anderson bringing country into the mix is a smart nod to the Texas broader audience. This isn't a lineup built to make national headlines — it's built to make the people who actually live in Houston feel like the Fan Fest is for them. That's the right call.

🏙️
City 05 of 16
Kansas City, Missouri

📍 Venue South Lawn, National WWI Museum & Memorial, 2 Memorial Dr, Kansas City, MO 64108
📅 Dates June 11 – July 11, 2026 (18 days)
🎤 Performers Tech N9ne + additional acts across 18 event days
🎟️ Admission Free (GA Pass — first-come, first-served, subject to capacity)
🔗 Official kansascityfwc26.com/fifa-fan-festival

This is probably the most architecturally interesting Fan Fest venue of the entire tournament. The south lawn of the National WWI Museum and Memorial — with the Liberty Memorial tower rising behind it and a commanding view of the Kansas City skyline — is a genuinely dramatic backdrop for a football festival. The city has hosted some of its largest ever public celebrations on this very ground. It knows what it's doing with a crowd.

Tech N9ne opening on June 12 is exactly the right booking — he is Kansas City's most internationally recognised musical export, and the decision to put a homegrown artist first (rather than a touring name who could have been booked anywhere) is a statement of civic pride. Kansas City doesn't have the profile of Dallas or Miami in most World Cup conversations, but its Fan Fest might be the most characterful in the USA. The venue alone is worth the visit.

City 06 of 16
Los Angeles, California

📍 Venue Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum, 3911 S Figueroa St, Los Angeles, CA 90037
📅 Dates June 11–14, 2026 (opening weekend) + additional match days
🎤 Performers Full entertainment programme TBC — check official site for updates
🎟️ Admission Free and open to the public
🔗 Official losangelesfwc26.com/fifa-fan-festival

The LA Memorial Coliseum has hosted two Olympic Games (1932 and 1984), five Super Bowls, and a papal visit. Using it as a FIFA Fan Festival venue for the opening weekend of the 2026 World Cup is a flex that only Los Angeles could pull off — the building is a century old and it still makes every event that happens inside it feel like a chapter in history.

The opening weekend (June 11–14) is where LA concentrates its energy, and that's the window to be there if you can. The Fan Fest hasn't locked in its full performer lineup publicly, which is a little unusual for this stage — but LA's production culture means whatever gets announced will be worth the trip. This is a city that doesn't undersell its moments. Watch the official site for lineup drops, which are likely to come closer to the opening weekend.

City 07 of 16
Miami, Florida

📍 Venue Bayfront Park, Downtown Miami, along Biscayne Bay
📅 Dates June 13 – July 5, 2026 (23 consecutive days)
🎤 Performers Lyanno Brytiago Juan Duque Mario Bautista Cris Cab Erick B
📐 Scale 436,000 sq ft · 10,000-cap amphitheater · up to 30,000 daily
🎟️ Admission Free; no advance ticket needed
🔗 Official miamifwc26.com/fan-festival

Of all the US Fan Fest venues, Miami's might be the most instinctively suited to this kind of event. Bayfront Park sits right on Biscayne Bay with the Miami skyline rising behind it — it's the kind of backdrop that makes everything happening in front of it look like a music video. The park has a 10,000-capacity amphitheater at its heart, and four-hundred-and-thirty-six-thousand square feet of waterfront space means thirty thousand people a day can be there without feeling compressed.

The performer lineup goes full Latin urban: Lyanno, Brytiago, Juan Duque, Mario Bautista — a roster of artists who have been defining the sound of Latin pop and urban music for the last few years. In a city where Latin music is the ambient frequency, this is the obvious move. Bayfront Park has also hosted some of Miami's most significant outdoor concerts, so the production infrastructure is already there. Twenty-three consecutive days on Biscayne Bay, watching World Cup matches with thirty thousand people and the ocean in your eyeline. That's a proper experience.

City 08 of 16
New York / New Jersey

📍 Venues Fan Zone Queens — USTA Billie Jean King Center (Jun 17–28)
Fan Village at Rockefeller Center, Manhattan (Jul 4–19)
📅 Dates June 17 – July 19, 2026 (two phases)
🎤 Performers Entertainment programming across both venues — check official site
🎟️ Admission Free and open to the public
🔗 Official nynjfwc26.com/fan-events
Important update: The originally announced Liberty State Park Fan Festival was cancelled in February 2026. The NY/NJ host committee replaced it with two distinct venues: Fan Zone Queens at the USTA Billie Jean King Center and the Fan Village at Rockefeller Center. Check the official site for the latest programming details.

New York's Fan Fest story is the most complicated of any host city. The Liberty State Park plan — which would have put a large-scale festival on the Jersey waterfront with the Manhattan skyline and Statue of Liberty in the background — was cancelled in February 2026. What replaced it is arguably more interesting, even if it's less photogenic as a single image.

Two venues, two phases. Fan Zone Queens at the USTA Billie Jean King Center covers the middle portion of the group stage — a 65-acre complex in Flushing that can handle serious crowd numbers and has the infrastructure from hosting the US Open year after year. Then the Fan Village shifts to Rockefeller Center for the knockout rounds: the NBC plaza, midtown Manhattan, one of the most recognisable urban spaces in the world. Watching a World Cup quarter-final in Rockefeller Center with New York around you — that is a thing worth experiencing.

City 09 of 16
Philadelphia, Pennsylvania

📍 Venue Lemon Hill, East Fairmount Park, Philadelphia, PA — over 1 million sq ft
📅 Dates June 11 – July 19, 2026 (all 39 days of the tournament)
🎤 Performers Luis Fonsi — Opening Night, June 11
🎭 Theme "Welcoming the World with a Philly Phlavor"
🎟️ Admission Free (registration required — reserve your pass in advance)
🔗 Official phillyfwc26.com/fifa-fan-fest

Philadelphia is running its Fan Fest for all 39 days of the World Cup, which immediately signals something: this city is treating this tournament as a full civic celebration, not just a series of match events. Lemon Hill in Fairmount Park is a stunning setting — rolling green grounds with the Philadelphia Museum of Art nearby and more than a million square feet to work with. It is genuinely enormous.

Opening Night brings Luis Fonsi to Philly — the man behind "Despacito," the most-streamed song in YouTube history at the time of its release, is a smart booking for a city with a huge Latin community and a Fan Fest with a clear Latin flavour to its programming. The "Philly Phlavor" theme signals that the city intends to inject its own personality into every element of the experience — cheesesteaks included, presumably. Register for your free pass ahead of time — this one is expected to be busy from June 11.

City 10 of 16
San Francisco Bay Area, California

📍 Venues Multiple distributed Fan Zones across the Bay Area — no single centralised Fan Festival
📅 Dates Running across tournament match days — check official site for location-by-location schedule
🎤 Performers Individual entertainment programming per Fan Zone — check official site
🎟️ Admission Free and open to the public
🔗 Official sfbayareafwc26.com

The Bay Area has taken a different approach to every other US host city: instead of a single centralised Fan Festival, it has chosen a distributed Fan Zone model spread across multiple locations around the region. This is less a logistical compromise and more a philosophical choice — the Bay Area is not a single city, and trying to funnel everyone through one focal point would have missed the regional character of the place entirely.

The trade-off is that the experience requires a bit more navigation. There is no single "go here" answer for Bay Area fans — the destination depends on which part of the region you're in and which matches you're watching. The official site has the full breakdown of locations and which zones are active on which match days. If you're based in San Francisco or Oakland, the distributed model will actually make the Fan Fest more convenient than a single venue would have been; the energy just spreads differently.

City 11 of 16
Seattle, Washington

📍 Venues "Unity Loop" — 4 distributed fan celebration zones across the city
📅 Dates Aligned with tournament match days throughout June–July 2026
🎤 Performers Live entertainment programming across zones — check official site
🎟️ Admission Free and open to the public
🔗 Official seattlefc26.com

Seattle has given its Fan Fest strategy an actual name — the Unity Loop — and built it around a network of four fan celebration zones distributed across the city. Like San Francisco, this is a deliberate departure from the single-venue model, and it reflects something real about how Seattle works as a city: it is a collection of distinct neighbourhoods with distinct characters, and the World Cup experience has been designed to live inside that geography rather than override it.

Seattle is also, to put it gently, a city that takes its football seriously. The Sounders fanbase is one of the most organised and passionate in MLS, and the city's football culture will be palpable during this tournament in ways that might surprise visitors expecting a casual sports town. The Unity Loop zones are free and built for fans who want to watch the matches properly — not just as a backdrop to a corporate activation. Check the official site for which zones are open on which days, and expect the crowd energy to be a step above what you'd find elsewhere.

🇨🇦 Canada — 2 Host Cities

🍁
City 12 of 16
Toronto, Ontario

📍 Venue Fort York National Historic Site & The Bentway, 250 Fort York Blvd, Toronto
📅 Dates June 11 – July 19, 2026 (22 event days, match days aligned)
🎤 Performers Bryan Adams Nora Fatehi feat. Sanjoy Vegedream AHI & Wyclef Jean
🎵 Special Countdown Concert — June 10 (eve of tournament), Grammy-produced
🎟️ Admission Free general admission; Countdown Concert separately ticketed
🔗 Official toronto.ca/explore-enjoy/festivals-events/fifa-world-cup-26

Toronto did something that no other host city did: it staged a dedicated Countdown Concert the night before the tournament began. June 10, 2026 — the eve of the first ball being kicked — and the FIFA Fan Festival grounds at Fort York were already packed. Bryan Adams headlining a Grammy-produced opening night, joined by Nora Fatehi (Moroccan-Canadian dancer and performer), Wyclef Jean in collaboration with AHI, and the French Congolese artist Vegedream. It was, by every account, a genuinely great night that set the tone for everything that followed.

The Fort York and Bentway setting is one of the most interesting in North America — a national historic site underlaid by the infrastructure of a city flyover, reimagined as a performance and gathering space. The combination of historic ground and contemporary architecture creates something that feels distinctly Toronto: layered, culturally rich, and entirely unbothered about what anybody else thinks is cool. Canada's Fan Fest has 22 event days tied to match days and a culture that will make every one of them feel worth attending.

City 13 of 16
Vancouver, British Columbia

📍 Venue PNE Grounds at Hastings Park, 2901 E Hastings Street, Vancouver, BC
📅 Dates June 11 – July 19, 2026 (28 days)
🎤 Performers Down With Webster + 120+ artist performances across 28 days
📐 Scale 10,000-capacity Freedom Mobile Amphitheatre · 100+ hours of live match broadcasts
🎟️ Admission Free general admission; headline amphitheatre concerts ticketed (from $44)
🔗 Official vancouverfwc26.ca/fifa-fan-festival

Vancouver's PNE Grounds are one of those spaces that the city takes for granted but visitors find remarkable. Hastings Park has been gathering Vancouverites together for over a century — summer fairs, concerts, exhibitions — and a 10,000-capacity amphitheatre is being built at its heart specifically for this tournament. Over one hundred hours of live match broadcasts, more than 120 artist performances across 28 days. The scale of the programming ambition here is significant.

Down With Webster headlining is a smart choice for a city that has a deep relationship with Canadian hip-hop and pop — they've been a festival staple across Canada for over a decade. The general admission model keeps the Fan Fest accessible, with ticketed headline shows at the amphitheatre for fans who want the full concert experience on top of the football. Vancouver in July is also, factually, one of the most beautiful cities in the world to spend time outdoors. The combination of mountains, ocean, and a properly resourced Fan Fest is hard to argue with.

🇲🇽 Mexico — 3 Host Cities

🌵
City 14 of 16
Guadalajara, Jalisco

📍 Venue Plaza Liberación — Guadalajara's historic central plaza, surrounded by the Cathedral, Teatro Degollado, and the Government Palace
📅 Dates Running across tournament match days through July 2026
🎤 Performers Full entertainment programme — check official site for announcements
🎟️ Admission Free and open to the public
🔗 Official FIFA Fan Festival — Guadalajara

Of all 16 Fan Fest venues in this tournament, Plaza Liberación in Guadalajara might be the most visually extraordinary. The historic central plaza is flanked by the Guadalajara Cathedral on one side, the Teatro Degollado on another, and the Government Palace behind — one of the most architecturally dense public squares in Mexico. Placing a World Cup Fan Festival inside this setting is either an act of tremendous confidence or an act of tremendous obviousness, depending on how you look at it. Either way, the result is spectacular.

Guadalajara is the birthplace of mariachi, the spiritual home of tequila, and a city with a football culture built around Chivas — one of the most passionately supported clubs in Latin America. The energy that this city brings to football is different from anywhere in the US or Canada. The Fan Fest here will feel less like a FIFA production and more like the city itself celebrating. That is, unambiguously, the better version of a Fan Festival.

🏛️
City 15 of 16
Mexico City (CDMX)

📍 Venue El Zócalo (Plaza de la Constitución), Historic Centre, Mexico City — one of the world's largest public squares
📅 Dates June 11 – July 2026 (throughout Mexico City's match schedule)
🎤 Performers J Balvin Maná Alejandro Fernández Belinda Tyla Los Ángeles Azules Lila Downs Danny Ocean
🎟️ Admission Free and open to the public
🔗 Official FIFA Fan Festival — Mexico City

The Zócalo. If you've been there, you already understand why this is the most compelling Fan Fest location in the entire tournament. One of the largest public plazas in the world, bordered by the Metropolitan Cathedral and the National Palace, with a history that stretches back to Aztec civilization — and now, in June 2026, it becomes the heartbeat of a World Cup fan celebration for one of the planet's great football nations. The symbolism is almost too much to process.

The performer lineup matches the ambition of the venue. J Balvin and Maná are global names playing to their home continent. Alejandro Fernández and Belinda represent the full breadth of Mexican pop. Tyla brings Afropop from South Africa — a nod to the global character of the tournament. Los Ángeles Azules and Lila Downs are Mexico's cultural soul rendered in cumbia and folk music. Danny Ocean bridges Latin pop and R&B. This isn't a booking list assembled by committee; it reads like someone who actually loves music made the calls. Mexico City's Fan Fest at the Zócalo is, simply, the must-see Fan Festival of 2026.

⚙️
City 16 of 16
Monterrey, Nuevo León

📍 Venue Parque Fundidora, Monterrey, Nuevo León — former steel foundry grounds turned cultural park
📅 Dates June 11 – July 19, 2026 (all 39 days)
🎤 Performers Chayanne Imagine Dragons Enrique Iglesias Grupo Firme + 21 artists total confirmed
🎟️ Admission General fan festival free; headliner concerts ticketed (via Ticketmaster MX)
🔗 Official fwc26monterrey.com

Monterrey has booked the most internationally recognisable concert lineup of any Fan Fest in this tournament. Imagine Dragons are one of the biggest stadium rock acts on the planet. Enrique Iglesias at a World Cup festival is cinema. Chayanne has been selling out arenas across Latin America and the US for over three decades. Grupo Firme brings the norteño energy that defines northern Mexico's music scene. Twenty-one confirmed artists across 39 days at Parque Fundidora — this is a music festival that also has a World Cup attached to it, not the other way around.

Parque Fundidora is a remarkable venue choice: the grounds of a former iron and steel foundry that has been transformed into one of Mexico's great urban parks, with industrial heritage structures still standing as permanent sculptures. There is nowhere else in the tournament where the physical location carries this combination of industrial history and natural beauty. Monterrey — Mexico's most economically powerful city and arguably its most modern — has built a Fan Fest that reflects its ambition. It is not subtle. It is also not trying to be.

Monterrey tip: The headliner concerts are ticketed separately from the free daily Fan Fest programming. Book through Ticketmaster Mexico at ticketmaster.com.mx — the Imagine Dragons and Enrique Iglesias dates especially are expected to sell out.
