Austria at the 2026 World Cup

Austria return to the World Cup after a 28-year absence — their first appearance since France 1998. They qualified by topping UEFA Group H, winning six of eight matches and finishing two points ahead of Bosnia and Herzegovina with a goal differential of +18. Under Ralf Rangnick — one of the most respected tactical minds in European football — Austria arrive not just as participants but as a team with genuine belief they can cause problems in North America.

The 1982 Elephant in the Room

Austria cannot escape the shadow of 1982 — and they shouldn't try to. In the most infamous match in World Cup history, Austria and West Germany played out a 1-0 result in their final group game that eliminated Algeria — a result both teams knew they needed to advance. FIFA subsequently ruled that all final group stage games must be played simultaneously — closing the loophole that made the 1982 conspiracy possible. Forty-four years on, Algeria are in Austria's group, and the ghost of Gijón still lingers.

Alaba, Arnautović and the Waiting Game

Captain David Alaba returns from the knee injury that ruled him out of Euro 2024 — he travelled to Germany as a non-playing skipper and watched from the stands. At 33, this is finally his first World Cup. He has won the Champions League four times — twice with Bayern Munich, twice with Real Madrid — and brings that winning mentality to a squad that has never experienced a tournament at this level. Marko Arnautović, 37, is Austria's all-time top scorer with 47 goals in 132 caps and arrives with something to prove after a difficult spell at Manchester United. Around them, Christoph Baumgartner carries the creative threat from midfield and Michael Gregoritsch leads the line with physical presence — the full attacking picture that Rangnick has spent three years building.

Group J: My Take

Argentina top this group. The real fight is between Austria and Algeria for second place — and I genuinely cannot split them. Both are capable sides. Both have a point to prove. Both know that the head-to-head on June 28 likely decides everything. Austria's qualifying form under Rangnick was dominant — six wins, one draw, just four goals conceded in eight matches. That defensive organization is going to matter. Jordan are the must-win opener to set the tone.

My prediction: Argentina first. Austria or Algeria second — the June 28 clash decides it. Austria have the squad to go deep if they get through. Under Rangnick, don't bet against them.
