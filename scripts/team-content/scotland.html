Scotland at the 2026 World Cup

Scotland ended a 28-year absence from the World Cup by qualifying for 2026 — their first appearance since France 1998. For a football nation that has been to eight World Cups and never once made it past the group stage, the weight of that wait is real. But the squad Steve Clarke has assembled is arguably the best Scotland have put together in decades — and the manner in which they qualified suggests a team that has genuinely turned a corner.

The Night Scotland Went Mad

The qualification moment came in a 4-2 win over Denmark that Scotland fans will recount for decades. Scott McTominay opened the scoring with a sensational overhead kick. Lawrence Shankland doubled the lead before Denmark clawed back to 2-2. Then Kieran Tierney fired home from 20 meters. Kenny McLean rolled one in from just inside the Danish half in stoppage time. Final score: Scotland 4, Denmark 2. It was the kind of moment that defines a generation, carrying Scotland to North America on a wave of belief that has not existed for a very long time.

The Full Circle Moment

Here is the detail that makes Group C genuinely poetic for Scotland fans. In their last World Cup appearance in 1998, Scotland finished bottom of their group — behind Brazil, Norway, and Morocco. Twenty-eight years later, Group C reunites Scotland with Brazil and Morocco, the same opponents who ended their 1998 campaign. Haiti complete the group. Scotland have unfinished business with two of their three opponents. That opener against Haiti in Boston on June 14 is must-win — get that result and the group opens up.

The Squad

Andy Robertson captains from left back, Scott McTominay has become one of Serie A's best box-to-box midfielders since joining Napoli, and John McGinn and Billy Gilmour add quality in the middle of the park. Craig Gordon at 43 is one of the oldest players at the entire tournament — still first choice in goal at Hearts and the calm, experienced presence this team needs behind a defense that will be tested hard. At the other end of the squad, 19-year-old Findlay Curtis joins after earning his first cap only in March — the youngest face in a group of players with everything to prove.

Eight World Cups. Never Past the Groups.

Scotland's goal is simple and has never been achieved — become the first Scottish side to reach the knockout stage of a World Cup. The Haiti game gives them the platform. Morocco will be a genuine test. Brazil is the mountain. But this Scotland squad has already done things previous generations couldn't. The Tartan Army have waited 28 years for this moment. They intend to make it count.
