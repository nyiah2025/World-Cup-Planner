**A Guide to All 16 World Cup 2026 Stadiums**

Sixteen stadiums. Three countries. One tournament unlike anything football has seen before. Capacities across these venues range from 45,000 to over 94,000, and together they'll host all 104 matches of the 2026 World Cup — from the opening whistle in Mexico City to the final at MetLife Stadium on July 19th.

One quirk worth knowing before we start: FIFA strips sponsor names from every venue during the tournament. The stadiums you know by their corporate names — MetLife, AT&T, SoFi, Lumen — all get neutral "host city" names for World Cup purposes, since the companies that paid for those naming rights aren't official FIFA sponsors. We'll use the names you actually recognize throughout this guide, with FIFA's official tournament name noted for each.

This isn't just a list of capacities. Some of these venues carry serious World Cup history. Others are engineering marvels built specifically to solve the unique challenge of hosting football in North American summer heat. And two of them sit at an altitude that could genuinely shape who wins this tournament.

---

**Mexico's Three Venues**

**Estadio Azteca** (Mexico City) — 83,000 capacity, FIFA tournament name: Mexico City Stadium.

There is no more historic venue at this entire World Cup. Opened in 1966, Estadio Azteca becomes the first stadium ever to host matches at three separate World Cups — 1970, 1986, and now 2026. It hosted the opening match of this tournament, just as it did in 1970 and 1986, making it the first venue in history to open three different World Cups.

The history here runs deep. The 1970 final was played at the Azteca, where Brazil beat Italy 4-1 in what's still regarded by many as the greatest World Cup final ever. Sixteen years later, the Azteca hosted the 1986 final too — Argentina's 3-2 win over West Germany. But perhaps the most famous moments in the stadium's history came in the quarterfinal earlier in that same 1986 tournament, when Diego Maradona scored both his infamous "Hand of God" goal and his "Goal of the Century" — a mazy 60-yard solo run past five England players — in the same match, on the same pitch.

Sitting at 2,200 meters above sea level, the Azteca's altitude is also a genuine competitive factor — more on that below.

**Estadio Akron** (Guadalajara) — 48,000 capacity, FIFA tournament name: Guadalajara Stadium.

The smallest of Mexico's three venues, Estadio Akron opened in 2010 and is instantly recognizable for its volcano-inspired exterior — a design echoing the volcanic landscape surrounding Guadalajara. It's the home ground of Chivas de Guadalajara, one of Mexico's most popular clubs. For the 2026 World Cup, it's also the only one of the 16 host venues that won't stage a single knockout match — group stage fixtures only. At 1,566 meters above sea level, it's the second of Mexico's two high-altitude venues.

**Estadio BBVA** (Monterrey) — 53,500 capacity, FIFA tournament name: Monterrey Stadium.

Nicknamed "The Steel Giant," Estadio BBVA opened in 2015 and offers spectacular views of the surrounding Sierra Madre mountains from its seating bowl. It's the home of CF Monterrey and sits at a far more modest elevation — around 540 meters — meaning altitude simply isn't a factor here the way it is at the other two Mexican venues.

---

**The Altitude Factor**

Here's something most fans outside Mexico have never had to think about: two of the 16 World Cup venues sit high enough above sea level to genuinely affect how football is played.

Estadio Azteca sits at 2,200 meters (7,200 feet). Estadio Akron in Guadalajara sits at 1,566 meters (5,138 feet). Every other venue at this tournament — including Estadio BBVA in Monterrey at roughly 540 meters — is low enough that altitude isn't a meaningful factor.

At elevation, the air is thinner. The ball travels differently — generally further and faster, since there's less air resistance. And for players whose bodies aren't acclimatized, fatigue sets in noticeably faster. FIFA has taken this seriously enough to regulate it: stadiums above 2,500 meters require a minimum acclimatization period for visiting teams, and that requirement extends to two weeks for venues above 3,000 meters. The Azteca, at 2,200 meters, sits just below that hard threshold — but the effect is still very real.

This is widely seen as a genuine home advantage for Mexico. Their deepest World Cup runs have historically come on home soil — the 1986 quarterfinal run included — and Mexican football officials have been candid about viewing the Azteca's altitude as "a very potent setting" for their own team. Visiting nations have spent weeks training at altitude specifically to prepare for matches there.

Interestingly, South Africa — Mexico's opponent in the 2026 opener — may be less affected than most visiting teams. Johannesburg's Soccer City, which hosted the 2010 World Cup opener, sits at around 1,750 meters itself. A team used to playing at altitude at home arrives at the Azteca with less of a shock to the system than a team coming from sea level.

**Canada's Two Venues**

**BC Place** (Vancouver) — 54,000 capacity, FIFA tournament name: Vancouver Stadium.

Opened in 1983 and extensively renovated since — including the addition of a retractable roof in 2011 — BC Place has serious pedigree on the world stage. It hosted the opening and closing ceremonies of the 2010 Winter Olympics, and in 2015 it hosted the Women's World Cup final, where the USA beat Japan 5-2 in front of a sellout crowd. For 2026, BC Place hosts seven matches — five group stage games, a Round of 32 match, and a Round of 16 match, the deepest knockout assignment of either Canadian venue.

**BMO Field** (Toronto) — 45,000 capacity, FIFA tournament name: Toronto Stadium.

BMO Field opened in 2007 with a capacity of under 30,000 — by far the smallest stadium in this tournament's history at its original size. A $146 million renovation added roughly 17,000 temporary seats to bring it up to the 45,000 required for World Cup hosting, still the smallest venue of the 16. It's the long-time home of Toronto FC and hosts six matches in 2026, including one Round of 32 game.

---

**The USA's Eleven Venues**

**MetLife Stadium** (East Rutherford, New Jersey) — 82,500 capacity, FIFA tournament name: New York New Jersey Stadium.

The stadium that gets the biggest prize of all. Opened in 2010 at a cost of around $1.6 billion, MetLife is the only stadium in the NFL shared by two teams — the Giants and the Jets — and has hosted some of the biggest events in American sports and entertainment, including Super Bowl XLVIII in 2014 and concerts from artists like Taylor Swift and Beyoncé. For 2026, it hosts eight matches across the tournament — and the eighth is the World Cup Final on July 19th.

**AT&T Stadium** (Arlington, Dallas) — 94,000 capacity, FIFA tournament name: Dallas Stadium.

The largest venue of this entire tournament. Opened in 2009 at a cost of roughly $1.3 billion, AT&T Stadium features a retractable roof and one of the largest column-free interior spaces of any building in the world. Its massive suspended video board — 160 feet by 72 feet — was a marvel when it was installed and remains one of the largest in sports. The stadium once hosted an NBA All-Star Game that drew a record attendance of 108,713 people. For 2026, it hosts nine matches — tied for the most of any venue — including a semifinal.

**Mercedes-Benz Stadium** (Atlanta) — 75,000 capacity, FIFA tournament name: Atlanta Stadium.

Opened in 2017 at a cost of around $1.6 billion, Mercedes-Benz Stadium is one of the most distinctive buildings in world sport. Its eight-panel retractable roof — inspired by the oculus of Rome's Pantheon — opens and closes like a camera aperture in under 20 minutes. The stadium also features a 360-degree circular video board known as the "halo board," measuring 58 feet tall and over 1,100 feet around. It hosted Super Bowl LIII in 2019 and is one of the few fully climate-controlled venues at this World Cup — a genuine advantage given Atlanta's notoriously hot, humid summers. It hosts nine matches in 2026, tied with Dallas for the most of any venue, including a semifinal.

**Arrowhead Stadium** (Kansas City) — 76,416 capacity, FIFA tournament name: Kansas City Stadium.

Home of the NFL's Chiefs since it opened in 1972, Arrowhead holds the current Guinness World Record for loudest crowd noise ever recorded at an outdoor stadium — 142.2 decibels, set in 2014 during a Chiefs game, breaking a record Seattle's Lumen Field had set the year before. For context, that's louder than a jet engine at close range. Arrowhead hosts six matches in 2026, including a Round of 16 match and a quarterfinal.

**NRG Stadium** (Houston) — 72,000 capacity, FIFA tournament name: Houston Stadium.

Opened in 2002 with a retractable roof, NRG Stadium is one of the most climate-controlled venues in the entire tournament — a serious asset in Houston's brutal summer heat and humidity. It hosted Super Bowl LI in 2017, the game famous for the Patriots' historic comeback from a 28-3 deficit. For 2026, it hosts several high-profile group stage matches, including fixtures involving Germany.

**Levi's Stadium** (Santa Clara, Bay Area) — 71,000 capacity, FIFA tournament name: San Francisco Bay Area Stadium.

Opened in 2014 in the heart of Silicon Valley, Levi's Stadium is the home of the San Francisco 49ers and hosted Super Bowl 50 in 2016. It's one of the more technologically advanced venues at this World Cup, reflecting its location.

**SoFi Stadium** (Los Angeles) — 70,000 capacity, FIFA tournament name: Los Angeles Stadium.

Opened in 2020, SoFi Stadium is widely regarded as the most expensive stadium ever built — estimates put the cost at around $5 billion. It's the shared home of the LA Rams and LA Chargers and hosted Super Bowl LVI in 2022. Its signature feature is a translucent canopy roof that shields fans from sun while allowing natural light and air through — distinct from the fully enclosed roofs at Dallas and Atlanta. For 2026, it hosts several major knockout matches including a semifinal.

**Lincoln Financial Field** (Philadelphia) — 69,000 capacity, FIFA tournament name: Philadelphia Stadium.

Opened in 2003, Lincoln Financial Field is the home of the Philadelphia Eagles and sits within the tournament's busy East Coast cluster of venues, hosting a solid slate of group stage matches.

**Lumen Field** (Seattle) — 69,000 capacity, FIFA tournament name: Seattle Stadium.

Opened in 2002 at a cost of around $430 million, Lumen Field was deliberately engineered to be loud — its clamshell roof design traps and reflects crowd noise back onto the field. In 2013, it set the Guinness World Record for loudest crowd noise at an outdoor stadium, reaching 137.6 decibels during a Seahawks game — a record later broken by Kansas City's Arrowhead. The roof covers around 70% of seats, meaning most fans stay dry regardless of Seattle's famously unpredictable weather. It hosts six matches in 2026.

**Gillette Stadium** (Foxborough, Boston) — 68,756 capacity, FIFA tournament name: Boston Stadium.

Opened in 2002, Gillette Stadium is the home of the New England Patriots and hosts several group stage matches as part of the tournament's East Coast cluster.

**Hard Rock Stadium** (Miami Gardens) — 65,326 capacity, FIFA tournament name: Miami Stadium.

Opened in 1987, Hard Rock Stadium is the home of the Miami Dolphins and has hosted more Super Bowls than any other venue in NFL history. For 2026, it hosts the third-place playoff alongside several high-profile group stage matches — fitting for a stadium so accustomed to hosting the biggest games in American sport.

**Roofs, Domes and Summer Heat**

A World Cup played across North America in June and July brings a challenge most previous tournaments never had to fully confront: serious heat. Temperatures in cities like Dallas, Houston, Atlanta and Monterrey regularly climb into the mid-30s Celsius during the tournament window, and FIFA president Gianni Infantino has been candid that covered, climate-controlled stadiums will be prioritized for daytime kickoffs wherever possible.

Only a handful of venues at this tournament offer full climate control — Mercedes-Benz Stadium in Atlanta and NRG Stadium in Houston are widely considered the gold standard, both featuring retractable roofs paired with full air conditioning that can maintain comfortable conditions regardless of what's happening outside. AT&T Stadium in Dallas also brings a retractable roof to bear against the Texas heat, though humidity around the natural grass pitch installed for the tournament has been a discussion point.

SoFi Stadium in Los Angeles takes a different approach — its translucent canopy provides shade and some protection from the elements without fully enclosing the venue, offering natural ventilation rather than air conditioning.

At the other end of the spectrum sit the open-air venues — Lumen Field in Seattle, Gillette Stadium in Boston, Lincoln Financial Field in Philadelphia, and Hard Rock Stadium in Miami among them. Seattle's Pacific Northwest climate means heat is rarely an issue there regardless. Miami is a different story entirely — open-air and exposed to South Florida's summer humidity, it's likely to be one of the most physically demanding venues for players at this tournament.

For fans, this matters too. If your team is playing a midday kickoff in Atlanta or Houston, you're watching in climate-controlled comfort. If they're playing midday in Miami, both the players and the crowd are in for a genuinely tough afternoon.

---

**The Final Word**

Sixteen stadiums, sixteen stories, one tournament. From the most historic ground in World Cup history at the Azteca — host to Pelé's greatest triumph and Maradona's most infamous afternoon — to the loudest crowds in American sport at Arrowhead and Lumen Field, every venue on this list has a part to play in how 2026 unfolds. Add in the genuine competitive wrinkle of Mexico City's altitude and the engineering response to North American summer heat, and this is a tournament where the venues themselves are as much a part of the story as the teams playing in them.
