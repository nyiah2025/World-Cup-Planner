Canada team page

Canada at the 2026 World Cup
This is Canada's moment. As one of three co-hosts, they'll play their group games in Toronto and Vancouver in front of home crowds that have been waiting a long time for this — Canada's last World Cup appearance was 1986, forty years ago. The squad Alphonso Davies leads into this tournament is a different proposition entirely from anything Canada has put out before. Davies, arguably the best left-back on the planet right now, is supported by Jonathan David — one of the most prolific strikers in Ligue 1 — and a core of Premier League talent that makes this a genuinely dangerous side, not just a feel-good host nation story.

What's at Stake
The draw could not have been kinder. Group B — Canada, Switzerland, Qatar, and Bosnia & Herzegovina — is the easiest group at the entire tournament. Switzerland are the highest-ranked team in the group at 19th in the world. Qatar and Bosnia are respectful opposition but not the kind of sides that should trouble a co-host with home advantage. If Canada don't make it out of this group, it would be a genuine shock.

Group B: My Take
I think Canada and Switzerland both advance comfortably, and I give Canada a real chance of topping the group. The key match is Canada vs Switzerland — whoever wins that sets the tone for everything else. Qatar showed in 2022 that host nations can compete but they were also eliminated in the group stage — only the second host nation ever to suffer that fate after South Africa in 2010 — and Bosnia at 65th in the world are the weakest side here by some distance.
My prediction: Canada first, Switzerland second. The Round of 32 should be the floor for this team — the real question is how far they can go after that.

USA team page


USA at the 2026 World Cup
This is already historic before a ball is kicked. The 2026 World Cup is the first to feature 48 teams — the biggest edition in the tournament's history — and the USA are co-hosting it on home soil for the first time since 1994. The atmosphere, the stakes, and the expectation around the USMNT have never been higher. Coach Mauricio Pochettino has built a squad around captain Christian Pulisic — arguably the most complete American player ever — and a young core with genuine top-level club experience across Europe's biggest leagues. After a Round of 16 exit at Qatar 2022, the pressure is on to go further, and the home crowd will make sure that pressure is felt.

Group D: The Path
USA are drawn alongside Paraguay, Australia, and Türkiye — a manageable group for a co-host ranked 16th in the world. Paraguay are the weakest side at 40th, and while Australia showed at Qatar 2022 that they can cause upsets, they are beatable. Türkiye are the most dangerous opponents in the group, sitting at 22nd in the world and boasting Real Madrid's Arda Güler — one of the most exciting young players in European football right now. The final group game, USA vs Türkiye in Los Angeles, could well decide who tops the group.

Group D: My Take
USA should advance comfortably, and playing two of their three games in Los Angeles with strong home support gives them a real edge. I'd take USA to win the group. Türkiye are quality but I think the American squad has enough to handle them. Paraguay I'm not too worried about. Australia are the interesting one — they're capable of a surprise and even if they finish third, the new 48-team format means the eight best third-place teams also advance, so they could still be around in the knockout rounds.
My prediction: USA first, Türkiye second. Australia might just sneak through as one of the better third-place finishers — don't count them out entirely.

Brazil team page

Brazil at the 2026 World Cup
No nation carries more weight into a World Cup than Brazil. Five titles — 1958, 1962, 1970, 1994, and 2002 — more than any other country in history. And uniquely, they have qualified for every single World Cup since the first one in 1930. Not once have they missed it. That kind of consistency over nearly a century of football is something no other nation can claim. Under legendary coach Carlo Ancelotti, Brazil arrive at 2026 with a squad built around Vinícius Júnior, Raphinha, and a supporting cast of elite European club talent. The pressure, as always, is enormous — but Brazil have always carried it better than anyone.

The Group C Opener Nobody Will Miss
Brazil's first game is arguably the most anticipated match of the entire group stage. On June 13 at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey — the same stadium that will host the World Cup Final — Brazil face Morocco. This is no warm-up act. Morocco became the first African nation in history to reach a World Cup semifinal at Qatar 2022, built on suffocating defensive organization, ferocious pressing, and the brilliance of Achraf Hakimi. Brazil will be the favorites, but Morocco will not be coming to make up the numbers. Scotland and Haiti complete the group, with Scotland returning to the World Cup for the first time since 1998.

Group C: My Take
Brazil should top this group — I'd be very surprised if they didn't. The real contest is for second place, and that's where Morocco come in as my pick. They've already shown at Qatar 2022 that they can go deep in a tournament and they won't be intimidated by the Brazil opener. Scotland are a decent side and will be competitive, but I think Morocco edge them for second.
The Caribbean in me is rooting for Haiti but they are the clear underdogs here.
My prediction: Brazil first, Morocco second — and both with something to prove in the knockout rounds.


Argentina team page

Argentina at the 2026 World Cup
The defending champions arrive in North America with one mission: do what no team has done since Brazil in 1962 — win it back to back. Argentina are ranked third in the world, have won the last two Copa Américas on top of their Qatar 2022 triumph, and coach Lionel Scaloni has kept 17 players from the title-winning squad. On paper, they are one of the two or three most dangerous teams in this tournament. The only question is whether they can do it again.

The Messi Situation
At 38 years old, Lionel Messi is heading to his sixth World Cup — a record he shares, fittingly, with his old rival Cristiano Ronaldo. Six World Cups. Let that sink in. His place in the squad was briefly in doubt after he left an Inter Miami game early with a hamstring issue, but Scaloni included him regardless. This is almost certainly his last World Cup, and you get the sense the entire Argentina squad knows it. Messi already holds the record for most World Cup appearances (26 matches) and most minutes played. If he lifts the trophy in July, there will be nothing left to argue about.

Can Argentina Do It Again?

[add attached picture here. caption "Lionel Messi lifts the trophy with Argentine teammates at 2022 World Cup finals / credit: REUTERS/Kai Pfaffenbach]
History says it's incredibly hard. No team has successfully defended the World Cup in over 60 years. But this Argentina side is not a one-tournament wonder — they have been the most consistently excellent international team on the planet for the past four years. The core is proven, the manager is elite, and the motivation with Messi's final chapter playing out in real time is unlike anything most squads experience.
I think they can. I'm not saying they will — but I wouldn't bet against them.

Group J: My Take
Argentina getting out of the group is a formality. Group J — Argentina, Algeria, Austria, and Jordan — is one of the more favorable draws a top seed could have asked for. Jordan are making their first ever World Cup appearance and are the clear weakest side. The interesting battle is between Algeria and Austria for second place. Honestly it's a toss-up — whoever wins that head-to-head match between them goes through.
My prediction: Argentina top the group comfortably. Algeria and Austria fight it out for second — could go either way.
