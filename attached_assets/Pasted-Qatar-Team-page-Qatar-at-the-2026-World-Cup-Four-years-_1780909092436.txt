Qatar Team page

Qatar at the 2026 World Cup
Four years ago Qatar hosted the World Cup. They lost all three group stage games — only the second host nation ever to be eliminated at that stage, after South Africa in 2010. And unlike South Africa, who at least went out with a win over France, Qatar didn't win a single game. It was a painful, very public introduction to international football at the highest level. This time is different. Qatar qualified for 2026 through the AFC qualifiers on sporting merit alone — the first time they have ever done so. Nobody gave them a spot. They earned it. 

How They Got Here
The road was anything but straightforward. Qatar began comfortably, winning their second round group with 16 points from six games. The third round was more turbulent — inconsistent performances before a crucial home win over Iran secured their passage into the fourth round. Then came the most dramatic moment of their campaign — a 2-1 win over UAE, with headers from Boualem Khoukhi and Pedro Miguel doing the damage, before Qatar were reduced to ten men in the 87th minute and held on through eight minutes of added time to seal qualification. That kind of pressure moment, navigated away from home soil, tells you something about how far this squad has come. 
Striker Almoez Ali — Qatar's all-time top scorer and the leading scorer in Asian qualifying with 12 goals — and creative winger Akram Afif are the two players to watch. Captain Hassan Al-Haydos, 35, brings experience and leadership with 188 caps — the most in Qatar's history.

Something to Prove
Group B — Qatar, Canada, Switzerland, and Bosnia — is a tough ask. Switzerland are the highest ranked side, Canada are co-hosts playing in front of home crowds, and Bosnia are no pushovers. Advancing from this group would be a genuine statement that Qatar belong at this level. The 2022 group stage exit still hangs over this team. Every game in 2026 is a chance to answer it.

South Africa at the 2026 World Cup
Sixteen years is a long time to wait. South Africa's last World Cup was on home soil in 2010 — and while they became the first host nation ever eliminated in the group stage, they left with one of the most iconic moments in World Cup history. 

YouTube embed: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HFISx1j0kak

When Siphiwe Tshabalala rifled home the opening goal of that tournament at Soccer City, sending 84,000 fans into delirium, it was the kind of moment that reminds you why football matters. Bafana Bafana are back — and they carry that memory with them to North America. 

How They Got Here
South Africa's qualifying campaign had a complicated middle section — they were handed an automatic 3-0 defeat for fielding an ineligible player, which cost them crucial points and put their qualification in jeopardy. They responded by winning their final game 3-0 against Rwanda, and results elsewhere went their way to see them finish top of their CAF group ahead of Nigeria. They finished with 18 points across the campaign — five wins, three draws, and two defeats. It wasn't pretty, but they got there. 

The Players to Watch
Captain Ronwen Williams is arguably their most important player — a goalkeeper who can keep South Africa in games they have no right to be in. In attack, Oswin Appollis is set to burst onto the global stage after years of quiet promise, while Lyle Foster of Burnley brings Premier League physicality and know-how. Veteran attacking midfielder Themba Zwane, 36, is the squad's most experienced outfield player — the calm head this team will need when games get tight. 

Underdogs With Nothing to Lose
Group A — South Africa, Mexico, South Korea, Czechia — is a tough draw on paper. But here is the thing about teams with nothing to lose: they can be the most dangerous ones in the room. One win plus a draw could be enough to advance, and South Africa could stand a real chance if Williams saves what he needs to save and Foster takes his opportunities. The new 48-team format means eight third-place teams go through — South Africa don't need to top the group. They just need to be competitive. 
Nobody expected Tshabalala's goal in 2010. That is kind of the point.

Ghana at the 2026 World Cup
There is a bittersweet note hanging over Ghana's 2026 campaign before it even begins. Mohammed Kudus scored the goal that sealed Ghana's qualification — a 1-0 win over Comoros in October — and is now ruled out of the tournament entirely with a quadriceps injury that required surgery. The man who got them here won't be there. That stings. But this Ghana squad has enough quality to make noise in Group L regardless, and the Black Stars arrive with something to prove after back-to-back group stage exits in 2014 and 2022. 

The 2010 Memory

Ghana's greatest World Cup moment remains the 2010 quarterfinal against Uruguay — and it remains one of the most painful too. They came agonizingly close to the semifinals before being denied by Uruguay in one of the most dramatic matches in World Cup history. Luis Suárez's infamous handball on the line, Asamoah Gyan's penalty hitting the bar, the penalty shootout defeat — an entire continent felt that loss. Sixteen years later, Ghana are back and hungry to finally go further. Wikipedia

The Players to Watch
Captain Jordan Ayew leads the side, with Antoine Semenyo — who joined Manchester City in January — and Iñaki Williams of Athletic Bilbao forming a quick, direct attacking line. Thomas Partey anchors the midfield from Villarreal, bringing experience and composure in the middle of the park. With 15 players making their World Cup debuts, this is a young squad with an average age of around 26 — fast, direct, and with nothing to fear. NBC New York + 2

Dark Horses in Group L
Group L on paper favors England and Croatia. But Ghana are the team I'd watch closely. Their opener against Panama is effectively a must-win — and if they get that result, everything opens up. Beat England or Croatia after that and Ghana are basically through. The new 48-team format also means a strong third-place finish could be enough. This group is more open than it looks on paper, and Ghana with Semenyo and Williams running at tired defenses late in games is a genuinely dangerous prospect.


Tunisia Team page

Tunisia at the 2026 World Cup
Tunisia are one of those teams that quietly go about their business while the bigger names get all the attention — and then they beat France in a group game and everyone suddenly remembers they exist. At Qatar 2022 they beat eventual runners-up France in their final group game, once again proving their squad is capable of competing with the very best. They went out despite that result, but the statement was made. This is their seventh World Cup appearance and their third in a row. The Carthage Eagles are not here by accident.

The Qualifying Record Nobody Is Talking About
Here is the stat that deserves far more attention than it is getting. Tunisia became the first team in World Cup qualifying history to reach the finals without conceding a single goal — ten matches played, zero goals against, 22 scored. Not once. In ten qualifying games. Under new coach Sabri Lamouchi, the defensive organization is not just good — it is historically unprecedented at this stage. A team that doesn't concede in qualifying doesn't just get lucky. That is a system, a mentality, and a squad that buys into both completely. 

The Players That Make Them Tick
Captain Ellyes Skhiri anchors the midfield from Eintracht Frankfurt, bringing 81 caps of experience and Bundesliga steel to the engine room. His partnership with Burnley's Hannibal Mejbri has made Tunisia a very difficult team to beat — Mejbri brings energy, creativity, and the kind of intensity that can unsettle bigger sides. Khalil Ayari and Rayan Elloumi are the emerging young names to watch — Elloumi at just 18 is the youngest player in the squad and could be one of the tournament's breakout names.

The Hardest Draw They Could Have Faced
Group F — Netherlands, Japan, Sweden, Tunisia — is brutal. On paper Tunisia are fourth favorites and I won't pretend otherwise. But a team that plays with this much defensive discipline and energy, that beat France at the last tournament, and that arrived here with a qualifying record nobody has ever matched before? They are not coming to make up the numbers. Tunisia are the kind of team that makes tournaments interesting — and if they catch Netherlands or Japan on a bad day, nobody should be surprised.

Japan Team page

Japan at the 2026 World Cup
Japan are one of my dark horse picks for this entire tournament — and I mean that seriously, not as a throwaway compliment. They were the first non-host nation to book their place at the 2026 World Cup, topping their Asian qualifying group with authority. Under Hajime Moriyasu they have beaten Germany, Spain, Brazil and England in recent competitive matches and friendlies — these are not flukes, this is a team that has figured out how to beat top opposition. The Samurai Blue play with an intensity, discipline and collective organization that makes them genuinely uncomfortable opponents for anyone. Nobody should be taking Japan lightly. 

The Mitoma Blow — and the Response
The squad announcement was overshadowed by one painful absence. Kaoru Mitoma, Japan's most creative and dangerous attacker, tore his hamstring in Brighton's match against Wolverhampton and was ruled out. When Moriyasu faced the press to announce his squad, his eyes filled with tears as he spoke about leaving Mitoma out. It was a genuinely moving moment — and it tells you everything about the culture of this squad. Without Mitoma stretching the wide areas, Takefusa Kubo of Real Sociedad becomes the primary creative force, supported by Ayase Ueda — Feyenoord's top scorer this season with 25 Eredivisie goals — and a midfield anchored by Liverpool's Wataru Endo. The depth is there. The squad adapts. That's what good teams do. 

A Record for the Ages
Veteran full-back Yuto Nagatomo, 39, becomes the first Asian player ever to appear at five different World Cups. That milestone deserves a moment. Five tournaments, from 2010 to 2026, across sixteen years of international football. He is the living bridge between the Japan that was still finding its feet on the world stage and the Japan that now beats Germany on a Tuesday night and nobody is surprised. Yahoo!

Group F: My Take
Group F — Netherlands, Japan, Sweden, Tunisia — is tough and Japan cannot afford to take any of these opponents for granted. Sweden have Isak and Gyökeres. Tunisia don't concede. Netherlands are the group favorites. Every game matters. That said, I have Japan second in this group. Their opener against Netherlands on June 14 is the defining match — if Japan can get a result there, the group opens up completely. Tunisia and Sweden are both winnable for a team of Japan's quality and organization.
My prediction: Netherlands first, Japan second — but if Japan beat the Dutch in the opener, flip those around. This is a team that is ready to go deep. Don't sleep on them.

Saudi Arabia team page

Saudi Arabia at the 2026 World Cup
If you were watching on November 22, 2022, you know exactly what Saudi Arabia are capable of. Arguably the biggest upset in World Cup history came courtesy of Saudi Arabia at Qatar 2022, when they took down eventual champions Argentina in the group stage. The world stopped. Argentina — with Messi, with a 36-game unbeaten run — beaten 2-1 by Saudi Arabia in one of the most extraordinary afternoons in football history. That moment proved something about this team that no amount of FIFA rankings could ever capture. On the right day, against the right opponent, Saudi Arabia can beat anyone. 

The Coaching Drama
The road to 2026 took a dramatic turn when the Saudi Football Federation sacked Hervé Renard on April 17, just weeks before the tournament, turning to Greek coach Georgios Donis as his replacement. Renard had overseen the Argentina upset and brought real credibility to the role — losing him so close to the tournament was a genuine shock. Donis was direct at his squad announcement: "I want to see my team from the first game very organized, very disciplined, to show our ambition." A new manager, a new system, and a World Cup starting in weeks. Saudi Arabia go into this tournament with more unknowns than most. 

The Players to Watch
Captain Salem Al-Dawsari — 2025 AFC Player of the Year — leads the side with 108 caps and the kind of big-game experience this squad needs. Firas Al-Buraikan leads the attack and finished as Saudi Arabia's top scorer in AFC qualifying. Almost the entire squad plays in the Roshn Saudi League, with RC Lens right-back Saud Abdulhamid the only European-based selection — an unusual concentration at a modern World Cup, but one that gives the squad a familiarity and collective understanding that more dispersed squads sometimes lack. 

A Group That Demands Their Best
Group H pits Saudi Arabia against Spain, Uruguay, and Cape Verde. On paper that is a brutal draw — two former World Cup winners and a debutant side with nothing to lose. Advancing would be a massive achievement. But Saudi Arabia have already shown the world once that paper means nothing when they cross that white line. The Argentina game will never be forgotten. The question for 2026 is whether this squad, under a brand new manager, can find that level again when it matters most.
They've done it before. That counts for something.

Czechia Team page

Czechia at the 2026 World Cup
This is Czechia's first World Cup in exactly 20 years — a return earned through one of the most dramatic qualification campaigns in European football history. They didn't cruise through. They overturned a 2-0 deficit against the Republic of Ireland to reach penalties in the playoff semi-final, then beat Denmark in another penalty shootout in the final. Two shootouts. Both won. That kind of nerve under pressure doesn't come from nowhere — it tells you exactly what kind of team Koubek has built. 

The Men in Charge
Head coach Miroslav Koubek, at 74, is the oldest manager at the entire 2026 tournament. He took over in December 2025 and his first two games in charge were those playoff matches. Thrown straight into the deep end — and he delivered. Patrik Schick leads the attack, with 25 goals in 52 appearances for his country and a Bundesliga title with Bayer Leverkusen in 2024 under his belt. West Ham's Tomáš Souček remains a pivotal player in central midfield — one of the most consistent and underrated midfielders in the Premier League for several years. Ten players come from reigning Czech champions Slavia Prague, giving the squad a collective understanding that more dispersed squads sometimes lack. 

The Group A Wild Card
Group A — Mexico, South Korea, South Africa, Czechia — sets up as a genuine four-way contest, and Czechia are the team nobody quite knows how to read. They are the only team in Group A that chose not to base themselves in Mexico, picking Dallas instead — meaning they face more travel than their opponents across the group stage. It's an unusual call. Their opener against South Korea on June 11 in Las Vegas is the match that defines their tournament — win that and everything opens up. Their final game is against Mexico at the Azteca, which is as tough a closer as any team faces in this tournament. 

Anything could happen in Group A. That's not a cliché — it's genuinely how this group is set up. And Czechia, a team that won two penalty shootouts just to get here, are exactly the kind of side you don't want to underestimate.
