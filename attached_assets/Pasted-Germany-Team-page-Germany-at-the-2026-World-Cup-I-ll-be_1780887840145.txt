Germany Team page

Germany at the 2026 World Cup
I'll be upfront — Germany have been my team since 2002, so I'm not exactly neutral here. Four-time World Cup winners, they are looking for their first title since 2014 — and after back-to-back group stage exits in 2018 and 2022, there is serious unfinished business. Under Julian Nagelsmann, Germany arrive at 2026 with a squad that finally looks like it has the quality and the identity to go deep again. Florian Wirtz, 23 years old, just finished his first season with Liverpool and has been a constant threat for Germany with 11 goals in 40 appearances. This is a team rebuilding with purpose — and 2026 feels like the right moment. 

Group E: The Curaçao Story
Germany's opener is against Curaçao — and that match alone is worth talking about. Curaçao are making their first ever World Cup appearance and will become the smallest nation ever to compete in the tournament, with a population of just 158,000. As someone from the Caribbean, I have a soft spot for that story. Their squad is built largely from players based in the Netherlands, organized, disciplined, and punching well above their weight just by being there. The rest of Group E is Cote d'Ivoire and Ecuador — both capable sides, neither a walkover. 

Group E: My Take
Germany top this group — that's the straightforward part. For second place I'd go with Cote d'Ivoire, who have the individual quality with Nicolas Pépé and Franck Kessié to edge out Ecuador. But here's the thing — Curaçao could genuinely sneak through as one of the best third-place finishers. The new 48-team format means eight third-place teams advance, and a disciplined, well-organized Curaçao side with nothing to lose and everything to gain could nick enough points to make it through. I'd love that for the Caribbean. I really would.
My prediction: Germany first, Cote d'Ivoire second — and don't rule out Curaçao as a third-place qualifier.

South Korea Team page

South Korea at the 2026 World Cup
South Korea are one of those teams I always enjoy watching at a World Cup. They play with a relentless energy and a refusal to be intimidated that makes them dangerous against anyone on their day. This is their 12th World Cup appearance and their 11th consecutive — a streak stretching all the way back to 1986. Their finest hour remains the 2002 tournament on home soil, where they reached the semifinals in one of the most dramatic World Cup runs ever. Under coach Hong Myung-bo — who captained that 2002 side — there is a sense that South Korea want to recapture some of that spirit.

Son Heung-min's Final Chapter
Son Heung-min, captain and talisman, is heading to his fourth World Cup at 33. After a decade at Tottenham he made the move to LAFC in MLS, and arrives at this tournament carrying a personal milestone chase — he is just two goals short of South Korea's all-time international scoring record of 58. This could be his last World Cup, and he arrives hungry. Alongside him, Lee Kang-in of Paris Saint-Germain and Bayern Munich's Kim Min-jae give South Korea genuine quality across the pitch — this is not a one-man team. 

Group A: My Take
South Korea come into this as arguably the strongest side in Group A after Mexico. They were the only Asian side to go unbeaten through qualifying — six wins and four draws — and that defensive discipline under Hong Myung-bo is going to matter. Mexico I'd still take to top the group, but South Korea second is my pick. Czechia are the danger — they are organised and hard to break down, and losing 3-2 to Ghana at Qatar 2022 in their second group game showed that no game is a given for them either. South Africa are the weakest side here.
My prediction: Mexico first, South Korea second — but watch the South Korea vs Czechia game closely. That one could flip the group.



Sweden team page

Sweden at the 2026 World Cup
Sweden probably shouldn't even be here — and that's exactly what makes their story one of the best at this tournament. They finished bottom of their qualifying group, were sacked their head coach Jon Dahl Tomasson, and replaced him with Graham Potter — a manager who had been out of work since leaving Chelsea. What followed was remarkable: Gyökeres scored a hat-trick against Ukraine in the playoff semi-final, then netted the 88th-minute winner against Poland to send Sweden to their 13th World Cup. They scraped through by the skin of their teeth and they could not care less. They are here. 

The Attack That Can Hurt Anyone
Once you get past the chaos of how Sweden qualified, what you find is a genuinely dangerous attacking unit. Alexander Isak and Viktor Gyökeres headline the attack — two of the most prolific strikers in European football right now, both playing at the highest level. Gyökeres finished the season as Arsenal's top domestic scorer. Isak has been one of the Premier League's best forwards at Newcastle. Having both available gives Sweden a front line that can genuinely trouble any defense in this tournament. Behind them, Lucas Bergvall gives Sweden Premier League quality in midfield at just 20 years old. 

Group F: My Take
Group F is Netherlands, Japan, Sweden, and Tunisia. Sweden are probably third favorites to advance but I wouldn't write them off entirely. Tunisia are the side they should beat — that game on June 14 in Monterrey is must-win for Sweden's campaign. If they take care of Tunisia, the group opens up. Netherlands are the clear favorites and Japan as I said on that page could sneak through. But here's the thing — Sweden with Isak and Gyökeres firing are capable of beating anyone on their day. They could sneak into second or even advance as one of the best third-place finishers.
Don't rule them out just because of how they got here. Sometimes the teams with nothing to lose are the most dangerous ones.

Türkiye Team page

Türkiye at the 2026 World Cup
Türkiye are back at the World Cup for the first time in 24 years — and they arrive with a squad that suggests the wait was worth it. Their last appearance in 2002 ended with a third-place finish — still their greatest World Cup achievement. Since then, nothing. But under Italian manager Vincenzo Montella, Türkiye finished just two points behind Spain in their qualifying group before edging past Romania and Kosovo with consecutive 1-0 wins in the play-offs. They are here on merit and they are here with serious talent. 

Güler, Yıldız and a Generation Worth Watching
This is the angle that has football fans excited about Türkiye. Arda Güler, 21, is the jewel of this generation — a Real Madrid playmaker whose left foot can unlock any defense. He led all Madrid players for chances created this season with 111, and was one of four players in their squad to reach 20 goal involvements. Alongside him, Kenan Yıldız of Juventus gives Türkiye a second genuinely world-class young attacker — and captain Hakan Çalhanoğlu anchors the midfield from Inter Milan. This is not a team built around one player. This is a team with a spine.

Group D: My Take
Group D — Türkiye, USA, Paraguay, and Australia — sets up beautifully. The game everyone wants to see is USA vs Türkiye in Los Angeles, and I have it penciled in as a must-watch from the moment the draw was made. I have Türkiye second behind the USA — but honestly, anything is possible in that matchup. If Güler is on form in Los Angeles, Türkiye could top the group. I wouldn't bet against it.
My prediction: USA first, Türkiye second — but circle that USA vs Türkiye game. It could go either way.



Curacao team page

Curaçao at the 2026 World Cup
This is the story of the tournament for me. With a population of just 156,115, Curaçao are the smallest nation by population ever to qualify for a World Cup — breaking the record previously held by Iceland. A Caribbean island of roughly the size of a small city, competing on the biggest stage in football. Just getting here is an achievement that deserves far more attention than it's getting.
What makes it even more remarkable is how they qualified — as the only undefeated team in the entire CONCACAF qualifying campaign. They topped their group in both the second and third rounds, going ten matches across the campaign with seven wins and three draws. This was not a fluke. They earned it.

The Story Behind the Squad
Curaçao have actively recruited from their diaspora, drawing heavily on players born and raised in the Netherlands — which explains the Dutch-heavy squad full of Eredivisie and Championship experience. Defender Joshua Brenet even played a World Cup qualifying game for the Netherlands in 2016 before switching allegiance. Captain and creative anchor Leandro Bacuna brings Premier League experience from Aston Villa and Cardiff City. All-time leading scorer Rangelo Janga carries the goalscoring burden. And then there is Tahith Chong of Sheffield United — one of the few squad members actually born in Curaçao itself.
The coaching story is something else entirely. Dick Advocaat, 78 years old and one of the most experienced managers in European football, guided them through qualifying before stepping down for personal family reasons. His replacement Fred Rutten then also stepped down with just a month to go. The FFK reappointed Advocaat to lead the squad for the tournament itself. If that's not a Caribbean plot twist I don't know what is.

Group E: My Take
Germany are the clear group winners and that's not the story here. The story is whether Curaçao can pick up enough points to sneak through as one of the best third-place finishers. Their games against Ecuador and Cote d'Ivoire are where it gets interesting — two very winnable points are available if the organization and discipline that carried them through qualifying shows up on the big stage.
I'll be honest — as someone from the Caribbean, I'm quietly rooting for Curaçao to make it to the Round of 32. The whole region would feel that. They won't win the group. But stranger things have happened, and this team has already proven everyone wrong once.
