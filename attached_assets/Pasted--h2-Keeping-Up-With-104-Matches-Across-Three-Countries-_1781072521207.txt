<h2>Keeping Up With 104 Matches Across Three Countries</h2>
The 2026 FIFA World Cup is unlike anything football has seen before. For the first time, 48 teams are competing instead of the usual 32 — which means 104 matches instead of 64, spread across 16 cities in three co-hosting countries: Canada, Mexico, and the United States. That's a lot of football. It's also a logistical puzzle for anyone trying to follow their team without missing a kickoff.
I built this site because that puzzle was frustrating me personally. Finding accurate match times in my local timezone shouldn't require a spreadsheet and some hope. So I made a tool that does it for you.

<h3>Four Timezones. One Tournament</h3>
With 104 matches running from Vancouver to Mexico City and Boston to Los Angeles, the 2026 World Cup officially spans four distinct timezones — which is where things get complicated if you're watching from outside North America.
<strong>Eastern Time (ET)</strong>
Covers the major media markets on the east coast — New York/New Jersey, Boston, Miami, Philadelphia, Toronto, and Atlanta. Most of the high-profile group stage matches are scheduled here.
<strong>Central Time (CT)</strong>
Home to Dallas, Houston, Kansas City, and Monterrey — plus the most historic venue of the entire tournament, Estadio Azteca in Mexico City. If your team plays at the Azteca, this is your timezone.
<strong>Mountain Time (MT)</strong>
Denver hosts a handful of matches — one timezone step behind Central, one ahead of Pacific. Easy to overlook, easy to get wrong.
<strong>Pacific Time (PT)</strong>
Los Angeles, Seattle, San Francisco, and Vancouver. Late kickoffs for European and Caribbean viewers — these are the games that start when most of the world is already halfway through their evening.
