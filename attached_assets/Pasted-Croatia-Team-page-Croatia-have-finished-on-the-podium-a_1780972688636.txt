Croatia Team page

Croatia have finished on the podium at each of the last two World Cups — runners-up in 2018, third place in 2022. For a nation of under four million people, that is one of the most remarkable runs in modern international football. Under Zlatko Dalić, the same coach who has guided them throughout this golden era, Croatia arrive at 2026 with one final mission — go one better. The squad is older, the window is closing, and everyone knows it. That makes them more dangerous, not less.

The Modric Farewell Tour
I love watching Luka Modric play. There is nobody quite like him — the way he controls the tempo of a game, the elegance, the vision, the relentless engine that somehow keeps running at 40 years old. This is his sixth World Cup — a record he shares with Ronaldo and Messi, making him one of only three male players in history to reach that milestone. He has 196 caps and could reach 200 appearances during this tournament. After nearly 600 games for Real Madrid and a move to AC Milan last summer, this is almost certainly his last dance on the biggest stage. Enjoy every minute of it. 
Behind Modric, Croatia have three other players with over 100 international caps — Ivan Perišić, Mateo Kovačić, and Andrej Kramarić. That is an extraordinary depth of experience for any squad. Joško Gvardiol returns after recovering from a broken shin — his presence at the back gives Croatia a genuine world-class defender at the peak of his powers. Young playmaker Martin Baturina, who impressed at Como in Serie A, is the name to watch as the next generation steps up alongside the veterans.

Group L: My Take
Croatia open against England on June 17 in Arlington — a rematch of the 2018 semifinal that England still haven't gotten over. That game sets the tone for everything. I have Croatia second in this group, but if Modric turns up in that opener and Croatia get the result, topping the group is absolutely on. England are the favorites but Croatia have beaten them before on the biggest stage and they will not be intimidated.
Ghana and Panama are the other two sides in Group L. Croatia should handle both. The real story is England vs Croatia — and I'll be watching that one very closely.
My prediction: Croatia second behind England — but if they beat England on June 17, flip that around entirely.


Panama Team page

Panama at the 2026 World Cup
This is only Panama's second World Cup appearance — eight years after their debut in Russia 2018, where they lost all three group games but scored their first ever World Cup goals in the process. They came, they learned, they went home. Now they are back, and this time they arrive with more experience, more belief, and a qualifying campaign that left no doubt they belong here. Under Thomas Christiansen, Panama swept through the first CONCACAF qualifying round with four wins from four, then went unbeaten through the second round — one of the most composed qualification campaigns in their history. 

The Alberto Quintero Story
Every World Cup has a story that reminds you why football matters beyond the results. For Panama in 2026, that story is Alberto Quintero. The 38-year-old winger was named in Panama's 2018 World Cup squad before injury ruled him out just days before the tournament. Eight years of waiting. Eight years of wondering what might have been. Christiansen handed him a second chance, and here he is — finally at the World Cup he was robbed of the first time around. He may not start every game. But his presence in that dressing room, and what it means to him and the Panamanian fans who know his story, is worth more than any tactical analysis.

The Players to Watch
Captain Aníbal Godoy leads from defensive midfield as Panama's record cap-holder — the heartbeat of everything this team does. Adalberto "Coco" Carrasquilla won the 2023 Gold Cup Best Player award and is one of Panama's most consistent performers in the middle of the park. Amir Murillo brings Ligue 1 experience at right back from Marseille — one of the few squad members playing at the highest European club level. Up front, José Fajardo and Puma Rodríguez were the joint top scorers during qualifying and carry the attacking burden. 

Group L and Beyond
Panama enter Group L as the lowest ranked side — but this squad is organized, hard to break down, and capable of a goal when an opening arrives. The opener against Ghana in Toronto is the game that defines their tournament. Win that, and the group opens up in ways nobody is expecting. Eight third-place teams advance in this format — Panama don't need to top the group. They just need to compete, pick up points, and make the most of every moment on the biggest stage they have ever known.
They waited eight years to come back. Don't expect them to waste a single minute of it.

Colombia Team page

Colombia at the 2026 World Cup
Colombia missed Qatar 2022 entirely — one of CONMEBOL's strongest sides failing to qualify was a genuine shock. They used that pain well. They came within extra time of winning the Copa América in 2024, losing to Argentina only when Lautaro Martínez scored in the 112th minute. They finished third in CONMEBOL qualifying with 28 points, edging Uruguay and Brazil on goal difference. This is a squad that has been building quietly and purposefully — and they arrive at 2026 as one of the teams I think could genuinely surprise people once the knockout rounds begin.

Díaz, James and the Final Chapter
Two players define this Colombia squad and both arrive carrying significant storylines. Luis Díaz scored 26 goals and provided 23 assists across all competitions for Bayern Munich this season — one of the most complete individual seasons of any player in European football. He is Colombia's undisputed leader and arguably the most dangerous attacker in Group K. Then there is James Rodríguez — 34 years old, playing in MLS, and somehow still the player who can change a game from nothing. He won the Golden Boot at Brazil 2014 and this is his final World Cup. He will not let it go quietly.
The big talking point around the squad is the absence of Jhon Durán — one of Europe's most in-form forwards at Aston Villa this season — left out by coach Néstor Lorenzo for disciplinary reasons. It is a significant absence for a team that can sometimes struggle to convert chances. But with Díaz in this form, Colombia have enough.

Group K: My Take
Group K — Colombia, Portugal, DR Congo, Uzbekistan — sets up perfectly as a dark horse opportunity. The Uzbekistan and DR Congo games should deliver six points — then comes the Portugal test in Miami on June 27. That is the defining match of Colombia's tournament. Portugal are favorites but Colombia with Díaz and James firing are capable of a result against anyone.
I have Colombia second in this group. Portugal are the class of the field here but Colombia are not far behind — and in the knockouts, a team with this much individual quality and a point to prove after missing Qatar entirely is exactly the kind of side that goes deep when nobody is watching. Dark horse of the tournament. I mean it.

Haiti Team page


Haiti at the 2026 World Cup
I'll be upfront — I'm rooting for Haiti. As someone from the Caribbean, seeing two Caribbean nations at the same World Cup for the first time is something special. Haiti and Curaçao both on the biggest stage in football, representing a region that rarely gets this moment. I want both of them to make the most of every second.

The Qualification Story Nobody Is Telling
This is the part that deserves far more attention than it is getting. Haiti's head coach Sébastien Migné has never been to Haiti. Safety concerns on the ground meant the Grenadiers used Curaçao as their home base for every single qualifying game — their own coach running the campaign remotely, never once setting foot in the country he was representing. In six qualifying matches, Haiti recorded three wins and two draws — topping their CONCACAF group with 11 points. Think about what it takes to do that. A nation dealing with real hardship off the pitch, playing every home game in another country, with a coach who has never visited. They qualified anyway. That is resilience in the truest sense of the word. 

52 Years in the Making
This is Haiti's second ever World Cup appearance — and their first in 52 years. Their only previous tournament was West Germany 1974, where they lost all three group games. Only two Haitians have ever scored in World Cup football — the most famous being Joe Gaetjens, who scored for the United States against England in 1950 in one of the biggest upsets in the tournament's history. Haiti have been waiting a long time for another chapter. This is it. 

The Players to Watch
Duckens Nazon is Haiti's all-time leading scorer and was instrumental in qualifying, scoring six goals including a hat-trick against Costa Rica. Wilson Isidor — a Sunderland striker who switched allegiance from France to Haiti and only made his debut in March 2026 — brings Premier League quality and pace to the attack. Jean-Ricner Bellegarde of Wolverhampton Wanderers drives from midfield — one of the squad's most technically accomplished players. Only one player in the entire squad, Woodensky Pierre, is based in Haiti itself — the rest are drawn from the diaspora across France, the USA and the Caribbean. A nation scattered across the world, coming together for this.

Group C and the Road Ahead
Group C — Brazil, Morocco, Scotland, Haiti — is as tough a draw as any team at this tournament received. Brazil and Morocco are both expected to advance. But Haiti's opener against Scotland on June 14 is genuinely winnable — and a result there changes everything. The new 48-team format means a strong third-place finish could be enough to advance. Haiti don't need to beat Brazil. They just need to compete, pick up points where they can, and show the world what this team is made of.
Being from the Caribbean, I want to believe they can do something special here. Get that result against Scotland, and who knows.


Australia Team page

Australia are making their sixth consecutive World Cup appearance — a streak that speaks to how far the Socceroos have come as a football nation. At Qatar 2022 they matched their best ever World Cup result by reaching the Round of 16, where they narrowly lost 2-1 to eventual champions Argentina. One goal away from the quarterfinals against the team that went on to win the whole thing. Under new coach Tony Popovic, Australia arrive at 2026 hungry to go one step further. 

A Squad Full of New Faces
Of the 26 players named by Popovic, 17 are heading to the World Cup for the first time. This is a squad in transition — experienced heads guiding a new generation. Captain Mathew Ryan earns his fourth World Cup appearance, equaling the Australian record held by Tim Cahill and Mark Milligan. His 104 caps are the most in the squad by a wide margin and his leadership between the posts gives Australia a foundation to build from. Jackson Irvine and Mathew Leckie each carry 14 international goals into the tournament — the experience and goals the attack will rely on.

The Two Stories You Need to Know
Cristian Volpato — a Sydney-born forward who had represented Italy at youth level — declared his allegiance to Australia just days before the squad deadline and earned the final attacking slot. One last-minute decision that could define a career. Then there is Nestory Irankunda — born in Tanzania to Burundian refugee parents and raised in Adelaide, the 20-year-old Watford forward is the most explosive attacking talent Australia has had in years. Pace, goals, and nothing to fear. He is the player opposing defenses will be planning for.

Group D and the Third Place Route
Group D — Australia, USA, Türkiye, Paraguay — is a tough ask for the Socceroos but not an impossible one. USA and Türkiye are the two sides expected to advance, and Australia are realistically fighting for third place. But as we discussed on the USA page, the new 48-team format means eight third-place teams go through — and Australia, with this squad depth and a coach who knows how to set up defensively, are absolutely capable of picking up enough points to sneak into the Round of 32. Their opener against Türkiye in Vancouver on June 14 sets the tone. Get a result there and the group opens up.
Qatar 2022 showed what this team can do when they believe. Don't write them off.

Uzbekistan Team page

Uzbekistan at the 2026 World Cup
Thirty-four years of independence. Eight failed qualifying campaigns. And then, on June 5, 2025, a 0-0 draw in Abu Dhabi that confirmed second place in their AFC group — and sent a nation of 38 million people into celebrations they had been waiting a generation for. Uzbekistan became the first Central Asian nation ever to reach a World Cup — and every game they play in North America this summer will be historic simply by existing.

34 Years in the Making
The road was not the easiest. The closest Uzbekistan came before 2025 was in qualifying for Germany 2006 and Brazil 2014, when they reached the final stage of Asian qualification only to fall short. The most painful moment came in 2005 — Uzbekistan beat Bahrain in an Asian playoff, but FIFA annulled the first leg over a refereeing error. The replay finished level and Bahrain went through on away goals. That kind of heartbreak could have broken a football program. Instead it hardened it. The qualifying campaign that finally delivered was built on defensive discipline — Uzbekistan lost only once across their 16 qualifying matches in rounds two and three, holding Iran to two draws along the way. 

The Coach, The Captain, The City Boy
The manager alone makes this story worth following. Fabio Cannavaro — 2006 World Cup winner and Ballon d'Or holder as a player, one of only three defenders ever to win the award — was appointed to lead Uzbekistan through the tournament in October 2025. He brings international tournament experience that no Uzbek coach could offer. Captain Eldor Shomurodov leads the attack with 90 caps, the most experienced outfield player in the squad. But the name that will travel furthest this summer is Abdukodir Khusanov — the defender who plays for Manchester City and brings Premier League and Champions League experience to a backline that will need every bit of it. 
The squad goes by the nickname the White Wolves — Oq Bo'rilar in Uzbek — the wolf being a symbol of strength and unity in Turkic culture. Fans have been rallying behind that name since the qualifying run began. Now the White Wolves are at the World Cup. 

Making History in Group K
Group K — Portugal, Colombia, DR Congo, Uzbekistan — is a tough draw for a debut. Portugal are one of the tournament favorites and Colombia are one of the dark horses. But the third-place route is real — eight teams advance from third place in this format — and a Uzbekistan side built on defensive organization under Cannavaro is exactly the kind of team that can grind out enough points to make the knockouts. Every game they play writes new history — they have never met Portugal, Colombia or DR Congo at a World Cup before.
They qualified against the odds. Don't put it past them to keep defying expectations once they get there.

Boznia & Herz Team page

Bosnia & Herzegovina at the 2026 World Cup
Bosnia & Herzegovina are at the World Cup for just the second time in their history — 12 years after their debut in Brazil 2014. And they did not get here easily. To qualify they had to beat Wales away in Cardiff on penalties in the semi-final, then face four-time world champions Italy in the playoff final. Italy took an early lead through Moise Kean, but after Alessandro Bastoni was sent off, Bosnia pressed and equalized with ten minutes remaining through Haris Tabaković. No more goals in extra time. Penalties again. Bosnia won 4-1 on penalties in Zenica — sending Italy home and themselves to North America. That is a qualification story worth telling.

Džeko and the Diaspora
Edin Džeko — nicknamed the Bosnian Diamond — is 40 years old, Bosnia's all-time top scorer with 73 goals and most capped player with 148 appearances. Despite his age, his form at Schalke in Germany's 2. Bundesliga — where he became the oldest goalscorer in the league's history — has kept him as a guaranteed starter. He and Sead Kolasinac are the only two survivors from the 2014 squad. Then there is the man who won the ticket — Esmir Bajraktarević, the 21-year-old PSV Eindhoven winger born in Massachusetts who scored the winning penalty against Italy. Born in the USA, representing Bosnia, scoring the goal that sends the mighty Italy — four-time world champions — home from a World Cup playoff for the second tournament in a row. Football writes the best stories.
Bosnia's squad is a diaspora project — eight players come from German clubs across the top three divisions, the rest spread across Italy, the Netherlands, Austria, and Eastern Europe. A nation scattered across the world, united for this.

Group B and the Opportunity Ahead
Group B — Bosnia, Canada, Switzerland, Qatar — is the easiest group at this tournament. Bosnia are realistically fighting for the third-place route rather than automatic qualification, but the new 48-team format makes that a very real possibility. Their only World Cup win came in 2014 with a 3-1 victory over Iran — they have never advanced past the group stage. Qatar are the team they should be targeting for points. A win there, combined with competitive performances against Canada and Switzerland, could be enough to make the Round of 32 for the first time in their history.
They beat Wales and Italy just to get here. Advancing past the group stage is the next chapter.

New Zealand Team page

New Zealand at the 2026 World Cup
They travelled further than any team at this tournament — 9,000 miles from Auckland to New York City — and they have done it before. This is New Zealand's third World Cup appearance, after 1982 and 2010, and the All Whites arrive with a quiet confidence that their previous tournaments never quite captured. They qualified directly via Oceania for the first time — no intercontinental playoff, no nerve-shredding second chance. They won their region outright, booked their ticket, and got on the plane. 

The 2010 Record Nobody Talks About
Here is a piece of World Cup trivia that deserves far more attention. At South Africa 2010, New Zealand drew all three of their group games — against Slovakia, Italy, and Paraguay — and finished the tournament as the only unbeaten side in the entire competition. Every other team, including the eventual champions Spain, lost at least once. The All Whites didn't lose a single game. They went home in the group stage on goal difference, but they went home unbeaten. That is the standard this squad knows it is capable of.

Wood, Smith and the Class of 2010
Chris Wood and Tommy Smith make history at this tournament as the first New Zealanders ever to appear at two men's World Cups. They were 18 and 20 at South Africa 2010 — now they arrive at 34 and 36. Wood captains the side and is one of only six New Zealanders ever to play in the Premier League. His Nottingham Forest experience and international goals record make him the focal point of everything New Zealand do going forward. Kosta Barbarouses, 35, who missed out on the 2010 squad entirely despite being part of the qualifying campaign, is finally at a World Cup after five attempts spanning fifteen years. His story alone is worth following. 

Group G and the Opportunity
Group G pits New Zealand against Belgium, Iran and Egypt. Belgium are the clear group favorites. But Iran and Egypt are both very beatable — and New Zealand need only one result to keep their third-place hopes alive. A win and a draw would likely be enough to advance as one of the best third-placed teams under the new 48-team format. The opener against Iran in Los Angeles is the game that defines their tournament. 
In 2010 they went home unbeaten. In 2026 they want to go home later.

Cape Verde Team page. 

Cape Verde at the 2026 World Cup
Seven previous attempts. Seven near misses. And then, on October 13, 2025, in front of 15,000 fans at Estádio Nacional de Cabo Verde, the Blue Sharks beat Eswatini 3-0 and a nation of 525,000 people celebrated the most historic moment in their football history. Cape Verde are at their first ever World Cup. With a population of around 525,000, they are the third least-populous nation ever to qualify for a World Cup — behind fellow 2026 debutants Curaçao at 156,115 and Iceland's 352,000 from 2018. Remarkably, two of the three smallest nations ever to qualify are both making their debut at the same tournament. As someone from small island nation, I'm always proud to see other small island nations making it to the biggest stage. "This country is small but the dream is big." That says it all.

How They Got Here
The qualification campaign was anything but straightforward. Cape Verde started with a goalless draw against Angola, then lost 4-1 to Cameroon — four-time African finalists — and a first World Cup seemed a distant dream. Then came the turn. A four-match winning run, combined with Cameroon dropping points against Angola and Eswatini, allowed the Blue Sharks to take over first place by a single point. On September 9, 2025, they beat Cameroon 1-0 — the goal scored by Dailon Livramento — to take a four-point lead. One month later they sealed it. Against the odds, against Cameroon, against everything. They got there.

The Players to Watch
Captain Ryan Mendes, 36, is Cape Verde's record scorer and most-capped player — the all-time face of Cape Verdean football, finally at his first and only World Cup. Logan Costa of Villarreal anchors the defence as the squad's lone representative from Europe's top five leagues — though his fitness is a concern after an ACL injury, with his return to action only coming weeks before the tournament. Dailon Livramento, 24, emerged as the qualifying campaign's breakout star with four crucial goals and arrives in North America with momentum and nothing to fear. Goalkeeper Vozinha — real name Josimar Dias — is 39 years old and the squad's elder statesman, still first choice between the posts at Chaves in Portugal's top flight.
Most players were born or developed in France, Portugal, the Netherlands and Ireland — a diaspora squad united by the Blue Shark badge. The coach, Bubista, is a former Cape Verde international himself who has managed the team since 2020 and delivered not just this World Cup but an AFCON quarter-final in 2023 along the way.

Group H and the Blue Shark Spirit
Group H — Spain, Uruguay, Saudi Arabia, Cape Verde — is as tough a debut as any first-timer could face. Spain are European champions. Uruguay are two-time world champions. Saudi Arabia beat Argentina. But Cape Verde beat Cameroon. They have already proven they can topple the expected. The Saudi Arabia game on June 26 is the realistic target — a winnable match that could earn Cape Verde their first ever World Cup point or better. The new 48-team format means even third place can be enough to advance.
Small country. Big dream. They earned the right to be there.
