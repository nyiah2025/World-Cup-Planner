Switzerland Team page

Switzerland at the 2026 World Cup
Switzerland arrive at their sixth consecutive World Cup — one of Europe's most consistently qualified sides of the modern era. Under Murat Yakin, who has signed a contract extension through Euro 2028, the Nati are not here by accident or tradition. They are here because they keep qualifying, keep competing, and keep going deep in tournaments that bigger names exit early. This isn't a flashy team — but it is organized, hard to beat, and used to deep tournament runs. 

Xhaka's Stage
Granit Xhaka holds the Swiss record for appearances with over 140 caps and captains the side into his fourth World Cup. His move from Bayer Leverkusen — where he won the Bundesliga title — to Sunderland in the Premier League has not diminished his importance to this team one bit. Xhaka is the metronome. Everything Switzerland do in possession flows through him. The retirement of Xherdan Shaqiri after Euro 2024 removed the one player who could produce individual moments of brilliance from nothing — his absence is the one real creative gap in this squad. Up front, Breel Embolo has overcome years of injury proneness to evolve into Switzerland's marksman, supported by Dan Ndoye of Nottingham Forest providing pace and directness from wide.

A History of Surprising People
Switzerland have a habit of making headlines at World Cups nobody expects. In 2006 they became the first team ever eliminated from a World Cup without conceding a single goal — going out on penalties to Ukraine after a goalless Round of 16. Then in 2010, they shocked eventual champions Spain 1-0 in one of the biggest upsets in tournament history. This is a team that quietly produces moments. Don't overlook them.

Group B: My Take
Group B — Switzerland, Canada, Bosnia, Qatar — is the easiest group at this tournament and Switzerland are the highest ranked side in it at 19th in the world. They should advance comfortably. Canada are the co-hosts and the real test — that match decides who tops the group. Bosnia and Qatar are both beatable. Switzerland's real tournament begins after the group stage, and a kind draw into the Round of 32 could see them go very deep.
My prediction: Switzerland second behind Canada — but if they beat Canada in the group, then it's the other way around. Either way, do not sleep on Switzerland in the knockouts.

Morocco Team page

Morocco at the 2026 World Cup
Morocco don't arrive at this World Cup as underdogs — they arrive with unfinished business. Four years ago they became the first African and Arab nation ever to reach a World Cup semifinal, eliminating Spain and Portugal along the way before narrowly losing to France. The Atlas Lions are no longer a surprise. They are ranked eighth in the world, they know how to beat the best, and they arrive in North America with one mission — go further than 2022.

A New Coach, The Same DNA
The big story heading into the tournament is the coaching change. Walid Regragui — the man who masterminded the 2022 semifinal run — resigned in March 2026 citing the need for a new vision. His replacement is Mohamed Ouahbi — a coach with almost no senior management experience but an extraordinary recent record. He led Morocco's U20 side to their first ever U20 World Cup title in Chile in October 2025, beating Argentina 2-0 in the final. Appointed directly from that triumph to lead the senior side with fewer than 100 days to prepare — that is a bold call by the federation. He did not concede a goal from open play in qualifying. The defensive DNA Regragui built is still very much there. 

The Players to Watch
Captain Achraf Hakimi — fresh from winning the UEFA Champions League with PSG and now the most decorated African player in history with 19 major team trophies — leads the side from right back. His fitness is the one concern after a thigh injury in April, but he was included in the squad with medical staff confident in his recovery. Brahim Díaz of Real Madrid is the creative heartbeat in attack, supported by Bilal El Khannouss and a midfield anchored by Sofyan Amrabat. Nine players return from the 2022 squad — the core that knows exactly what knockout football at this level demands. 

Group C: My Take
Group C opens with Morocco vs Brazil on June 13 at MetLife Stadium — the same stadium hosting the World Cup Final. That is the defining match not just for this group but for Morocco's entire tournament. Brazil are favorites but Morocco beat Spain and Portugal in 2022. They are not going to MetLife to sit back and absorb pressure. Scotland and Haiti complete the group — both beatable for a side of Morocco's quality.
I have Morocco second behind Brazil. But if they get a result in that opener, everything changes. Morocco are one of the teams I think could go very deep in this tournament — and this time, nobody will be surprised if they do.


Scotland Team page


Scotland at the 2026 World Cup
Scotland ended a 28-year absence from the World Cup by qualifying for 2026 — their first appearance since France 1998. For a football nation that has been to eight World Cups and never once made it past the group stage, the weight of that wait is real. But the squad Steve Clarke has assembled is arguably the best Scotland have put together in decades — and the manner in which they qualified suggests a team that has genuinely turned a corner.

The Night Scotland Went Mad

The qualification moment came in a 4-2 win over Denmark that Scotland fans will recount for decades. Scott McTominay opened the scoring with a sensational overhead kick. Lawrence Shankland doubled the lead before Denmark clawed back to 2-2. Then Kieran Tierney fired home from 20 meters. Kenny McLean rolled one in from just inside the Danish half in stoppage time. Final score: Scotland 4, Denmark 2. It was the kind of moment that defines a generation, carrying Scotland to North America on a wave of belief that has not existed for a very long time. 

The Full Circle Moment
Here is the detail that makes Group C genuinely poetic for Scotland fans. In their last World Cup appearance in 1998, Scotland finished bottom of their group — behind Brazil, Norway, and Morocco. Twenty-eight years later, Group C reunites Scotland with Brazil and Morocco, the same opponents who ended their 1998 campaign. Haiti complete the group. Scotland have unfinished business with two of their three opponents. That opener against Haiti in Boston on June 14 is must-win — get that result and the group opens up.

The Squad
Andy Robertson captains from left back, Scott McTominay has become one of Serie A's best box-to-box midfielders since joining Napoli, and John McGinn and Billy Gilmour add quality in the middle of the park. Craig Gordon at 43 is one of the oldest players at the entire tournament — still first choice in goal at Hearts and the calm, experienced presence this team needs behind a defense that will be tested hard. At the other end of the squad, 19-year-old Findlay Curtis joins after earning his first cap only in March — the youngest face in a group of players with everything to prove.

Eight World Cups. Never Past the Groups.
Scotland's goal is simple and has never been achieved — become the first Scottish side to reach the knockout stage of a World Cup. The Haiti game gives them the platform. Morocco will be a genuine test. Brazil is the mountain. But this Scotland squad has already done things previous generations couldn't. The Tartan Army have waited 28 years for this moment. They intend to make it count.


Paraguay Team page

Paraguay at the 2026 World Cup
Paraguay are back at the World Cup for the first time in 16 years — returning to the stage where their greatest ever moment happened. In 2010 in South Africa, Los Guaraníes reached the quarterfinals for the first time in their history before bowing out to eventual champions Spain. Since then — nothing. Three consecutive failed qualifying campaigns. A nation of passionate football supporters left watching from the outside. That changes in 2026. 

The Comeback Story
The road to North America nearly didn't happen. Paraguay took just one point from their first three qualifying matches and sat near the bottom of the CONMEBOL table. Then Gustavo Alfaro arrived in August 2024 and everything changed. La Albirroja stunned Brazil 1-0 in Asunción through a Diego Gómez strike, then beat Argentina 2-1 with goals from Antonio Sanabria and Omar Alderete. Beating both Brazil and Argentina in the same qualifying campaign — from a position where they looked dead in the water — is one of the great CONMEBOL qualifying stories in recent memory. They finished sixth with 28 points, the same number as Brazil, claiming the final automatic spot. 

The Players to Watch
Julio Enciso is the player who makes Paraguay tick — the 22-year-old Strasbourg attacking midfielder contributed 12 goals and 10 assists across all competitions this season and is not afraid to shoot from anywhere. Miguel Almirón — back in MLS with Atlanta United after his years at Newcastle — brings experience and creativity in the number ten role. Captain Gustavo Gómez anchors the defense with over 88 caps, while Omar Alderete has been making waves in the Premier League at Sunderland. Veteran goalkeeper Roberto Fernández, 37, is acutely aware this may be his final World Cup — that kind of motivation runs through this entire squad. 

Defense First, Always
Paraguay are a team known for their defensive discipline and being very hard to beat — they conceded just 10 goals across the entire CONMEBOL qualifying campaign. Group D — USA, Türkiye, Australia, Paraguay — is competitive but not impossible. The USA opener in Los Angeles is the toughest test. But a team that beat Brazil and Argentina in qualifying is not going to be intimidated by anyone. Paraguay play all three group games in California — one corner of the country, minimal travel, maximum focus.
They came back from the dead to qualify. Don't expect them to come to North America just to make up the numbers.


Cote d'Ivoire Team page


Côte d'Ivoire at the 2026 World Cup
Côte d'Ivoire are back at the World Cup for the first time since 2014 — ending a 12-year absence that began with one of the most heartbreaking eliminations in tournament history. At Brazil 2014, the Elephants beat Japan in their opener and needed only a draw against Greece in their final group game to advance. Deep into stoppage time, a penalty conceded inside the box led to a dramatic 2-1 defeat that eliminated them on the cruelest possible terms. Twelve years of hurt. They are back, and they have unfinished business. 

The Coach Who Was There in 2006
The man leading them back carries a personal piece of this history. Emerse Faé played for Côte d'Ivoire in their World Cup debut in 2006 — the first time the Elephants ever appeared at a tournament. Twenty years later he is the coach. He took over mid-tournament at the 2023 AFCON after the sacking of Jean-Louis Gasset and guided Côte d'Ivoire to the title — their third ever. That ability to step in cold and deliver under pressure is exactly the kind of character this squad needs on the touchline. 

The Squad
Franck Kessié, the century-cap captain and midfield heartbeat, holds everything together. Ousmane Diomandé, Evan Ndicka and Wilfried Singo form a formidable defensive unit, while in attack Amad Diallo, Nicolas Pépé and the emerging Yan Diomandé give Côte d'Ivoire one of the most exciting attacking options at the tournament. They topped CAF Group F in qualifying with an unbeaten record — eight wins, two draws, and a goal difference of +25, the best in African qualification. This is not a team that scraped through. They dominated their continent. 

Group E and the Curse to Break
Côte d'Ivoire have appeared at three previous World Cups — 2006, 2010, 2014 — and exited in the group stage every single time. Group E — Germany, Ecuador, Curaçao, Côte d'Ivoire — gives them the opportunity to finally break that curse. Germany are the group favorites but Ecuador and Curaçao are both very winnable. A squad of this quality, under a coach who knows what this tournament means personally, arriving on the back of an unbeaten qualifying campaign — this feels like the year the Elephants finally go further. 
Twelve years is long enough to wait. The 2014 penalty still stings. Time to write a different ending.


Ecuador Team page

Ecuador at the 2026 World Cup
Ecuador arrive at the 2026 World Cup carrying the hopes of a nation that has quietly assembled what many are calling the most talented generation of players in their history. This is their fifth World Cup appearance — their finest hour coming in 2006 when they reached the Round of 16 before a narrow 1-0 defeat to England. Two decades on, the expectation is that this squad can go further. Much further. 

The Qualifying Story
Ecuador started the CONMEBOL qualifying campaign in a hole — FIFA docked them three points for using falsified birth documents for a player in the previous cycle. They absorbed that penalty, won their opening match against Venezuela, and never looked back. They finished second in CONMEBOL qualifying behind only defending champions Argentina — ahead of Brazil, Colombia and Uruguay. They lost just twice away from home across the entire campaign — against Argentina and Brazil — while conceding only five goals in 18 matches, the best defensive record in South America. A three-point deduction, an opening loss, and still second in the continent. That is a statement. 

The Golden Generation
Two players define this Ecuador squad. Moisés Caicedo anchors midfield at Chelsea — one of the most expensive midfielders in the game and Ecuador's engine in every sense. Behind him, Piero Hincapié of Arsenal and Willian Pacho of PSG form a center-back partnership that is as good as any defensive pairing at this tournament. Then there is the future — 18-year-old Kendry Páez, now at River Plate, is the most exciting young talent in Ecuadorian football and arrives in North America ready to announce himself to the world. Captain Enner Valencia at 36 leads the line one final time — the all-time top scorer for his country and the player who has carried this team on his back for a decade. 

Group E: The Opportunity
Group E — Germany, Côte d'Ivoire, Curaçao, Ecuador — is very much within reach. Ecuador's style under Beccacece is defensive discipline and dangerous counter-attacks — exactly the kind of football that can frustrate Germany and beat Côte d'Ivoire on a good day. Curaçao are the must-win game. The final group match against Germany at MetLife Stadium on June 25 could decide who advances — and a team that beat Argentina at home in qualifying is not going to be intimidated by anyone.
This Golden Generation has only ever reached the knockout stage once. 2026 is the year they intend to change that.

Belgium Team page

Belgium at the 2026 World Cup
This is almost certainly the last World Cup for Belgium's golden generation — Kevin De Bruyne, Romelu Lukaku and Thibaut Courtois all named in the squad for one final shot at the major prize that the most talented group in Belgian history never won. That generation peaked in Russia 2018 and crashed out in Qatar 2022 without a single knockout match. The window is closing. 2026 is the last realistic chance to make history — and this squad knows it. 

One Last Chance for the Golden Generation
Belgium twice lost out to the eventual winners in the semifinals — Argentina in 1986 and France in 2018 — before beating England for third place in the latter tournament. Their best is a bronze medal. De Bruyne at 34, now at Napoli after leaving Manchester City, arrives fit and with his passing range fully intact — operating as the number 10 in Garcia's 4-2-3-1, if Belgium go deep, De Bruyne pulling the strings will be the reason. Lukaku enters the tournament with 89 international goals — Belgium's all-time record scorer — despite a season disrupted by injury. Courtois in goal is among the best in the world on his best days. De Bruyne, Courtois, Lukaku and Witsel all appear at their fourth successive World Cup together — a statistic that underlines just how long this generation has been together and how much they still want this. 

The Squad Controversy
The loudest reaction to Garcia's squad announcement came from a notable absence — Malick Fofana, Belgium's top scorer in UEFA qualifying with six goals, was left out entirely. A bold call from a manager who clearly decided the veterans deserved their final shot over the emerging generation. Time will tell if that call is vindicated.

Group G: My Take
Group G — Belgium, Egypt, Iran, New Zealand — is about as kind a draw as Belgium could have hoped for. Egypt bring defensive organization and are the toughest test on paper. Iran have seven World Cup appearances and a disciplined structure. New Zealand are the group's biggest underdogs. Belgium should top this group — I'd be genuinely surprised if they didn't. The Egypt opener on June 15 in Seattle is the one to watch. If Belgium are sharp there the group is done. They beat the United States 5-2 in Atlanta in a March warm-up with De Bruyne and Lukaku both fit and clicking — that performance showed exactly what this team is capable of when everything clicks. 
My prediction: Belgium first. The golden generation deserves one more run. This is it.


Egypt Team page

Egypt at the 2026 World Cup
Egypt will be appearing at their fourth FIFA World Cup — a surprisingly low number given their status as an African football powerhouse. But the history runs deep. In 1934 Egypt became the first African and Middle Eastern nation ever to appear at a World Cup — a milestone that predates almost every other African football story by decades. Since then their World Cup record has been modest — seven matches, zero wins, two draws, five losses. That changes in 2026. This squad, under this coach, with this captain, is built to finally win a World Cup match. 

Salah, Hassan and the Record Chase
The story within the story is Mohamed Salah chasing his own coach's record. Hossam Hassan is Egypt's all-time top scorer — and Salah, closing in with 67 international goals, may have something to say about that before this tournament is over. Salah scored nine goals in qualifying — more than any other player in the group — and turns 34 on the very day Egypt face Belgium in their opener. What a birthday present it would be to beat them. Omar Marmoush of Manchester City gives Egypt a second genuine threat alongside Salah — pace, movement, and the ability to stretch any defense that crowds out the captain. 

The Coach
Hossam Hassan is an Egyptian football legend — a three-time Africa Cup of Nations winner as a player whose career spanned over two decades. He took over the national team in early 2024 and guided Egypt through an unbeaten qualifying campaign — eight wins and two draws across ten matches with a +18 goal differential. Egypt reached the Africa Cup of Nations semi-finals in the build-up to this tournament — the form and momentum are genuine heading into Group G. 

Group G and the Knockout Dream
Egypt have never reached the knockout rounds of a World Cup — and with Belgium, New Zealand and Iran in Group G, this is their best realistic chance to change that. Belgium are the group favorites but Egypt's defensive organization and Salah's quality make them a genuine threat for second place. New Zealand are the most beatable side in the group. Iran are organized and hard to break down. The Iran game on June 27 could well decide who goes through alongside Belgium.
The Pharaohs have zero World Cup wins from seven matches. Salah's final World Cup deserves a different story. All of Egypt believes this is the year it gets written.


Iran Team page


Iran at the 2026 World Cup
Six World Cups. Six group stage exits. That is the record Iran carries into North America — and this squad is determined to finally rewrite it. This is Iran's seventh World Cup and their fourth in a row — a consistency of qualification that reflects genuine football development across the country. Ranked 21st in the world, Iran arrive in Group G alongside Belgium, Egypt and New Zealand — and for the first time in a long time, the draw feels genuinely kind.

The Off-Pitch Story
Iran head to North America with relations strained as US President Donald Trump takes a hard line over nuclear talks with Tehran. FIFA approved Iran's request to move their World Cup training camp from the United States to Mexico — a small but telling detail about the political backdrop surrounding this squad. Whatever happens off the pitch, the players have earned their place here and deserve to be judged on what they do on it.

Taremi and the Senior Core
Captain Mehdi Taremi has netted 59 times in 103 international appearances — one of the most prolific international strikers in Asian football history. At 33 and in his third World Cup, he scored twice against England at Qatar 2022 in one of the most memorable individual performances by an Iranian player at a World Cup. He scored 10 goals in qualifying. Everything Iran do going forward flows through him. This is a veteran group — one of the three oldest squads at the entire tournament — with Alireza Jahanbakhsh and Saman Ghoddos bringing European experience from their time at Brighton and Brentford respectively. 

Group G and the Knockout Dream
The draw is kind by Iranian standards and coach Ghalenoei knows this is a real chance to reach the knockouts for the first time. Belgium are the group favorites and a tough opening test. But the Egypt game on June 27 in Seattle is the one that matters most — that is effectively a playoff for second place. New Zealand are the most beatable side in the group. Iran's counter-attacking, defensively disciplined style under Ghalenoei is exactly the kind of football that can grind out results when it matters. 
Six World Cups without making the knockout stage. Group G in 2026 is the most realistic opportunity Iran have ever had to change that. Taremi and this experienced squad know it — and they will not waste it.



Uruguay Team page

Uruguay at the 2026 World Cup
Two-time world champions. Chasing their first title in over 75 years. Uruguay arrive at 2026 as one of my genuine dark horse picks — a team with the history, the talent, and a manager in Marcelo Bielsa whose methods are as demanding as anyone in world football. Ranked 17th in the world, this is their 15th FIFA World Cup — a participation record that speaks to how consistently Uruguay have competed at the highest level despite being a nation of just 3.5 million people. 

The Rich History
Uruguay last won the World Cup in 1950 — the famous Maracanazo, when they stunned Brazil in front of nearly 200,000 people at the Maracanã in the deciding match to claim the title. They also won the inaugural 1930 tournament on home soil. No South American nation has more World Cup titles per capita. The semifinal run at South Africa 2010 remains their finest modern hour — a tournament where Luis Suárez, Diego Forlán, and a steel-hard defensive unit reminded the world that Uruguay are never just making up the numbers. 

Suárez Out, Muslera Back
The two biggest squad stories both involve veterans. Luis Suárez — Uruguay's all-time top scorer — was left out by Bielsa despite being fit and in form at Inter Miami, missing a World Cup for the first time since 2010. A bold, ruthless call that tells you everything about how Bielsa operates. Meanwhile, Fernando Muslera came out of international retirement at 39 to chase a fifth World Cup — a Uruguayan record. The veteran Galatasaray goalkeeper, 133 caps deep, decided he wasn't done yet. 

The Squad
Federico Valverde is the heartbeat of this Uruguay side — one of the most complete midfielders in world football, with multiple Champions League titles at Real Madrid and a Ballon d'Or top-five finish. Ronald Araújo and José María Giménez form one of the world's elite center-back pairings — Bielsa's entire philosophy is built around their ability to defend high lines and recover quickly. Darwin Núñez leads the attack, having topped La Celeste's qualifying scorer chart with five goals. Manuel Ugarte and Rodrigo Bentancur provide Premier League steel in midfield. 

Group H: My Take
Uruguay beat both Brazil and Argentina in qualifying — that is the kind of statement that doesn't happen by accident. Group H — Spain, Uruguay, Saudi Arabia, Cape Verde — sets up perfectly for a deep run. Saudi Arabia are the must-win opener. Cape Verde are dangerous but beatable. Then comes Spain on the final matchday in Guadalajara — and whoever wins that game almost certainly tops the group. I have Uruguay second behind Spain, but that Spain vs Uruguay closer could be one of the matches of the group stage. 
My prediction: Spain first, Uruguay second — and don't be surprised if Uruguay are still standing deep into the knockout rounds.
