Austria Team page

Austria at the 2026 World Cup
Austria return to the World Cup after a 28-year absence — their first appearance since France 1998. They qualified by topping UEFA Group H, winning six of eight matches and finishing two points ahead of Bosnia and Herzegovina with a goal differential of +18. Under Ralf Rangnick — one of the most respected tactical minds in European football — Austria arrive not just as participants but as a team with genuine belief they can cause problems in North America.

The 1982 Elephant in the Room
Austria cannot escape the shadow of 1982 — and they shouldn't try to. In the most infamous match in World Cup history, Austria and West Germany played out a 1-0 result in their final group game that eliminated Algeria — a result both teams knew they needed to advance. FIFA subsequently ruled that all final group stage games must be played simultaneously — closing the loophole that made the 1982 conspiracy possible. Forty-four years on, Algeria are in Austria's group, and the final group game between them in Kansas City on June 28 is the most historically loaded fixture of the entire group stage. Austria's players weren't born in 1982. But football has a long memory.

Alaba, Arnautović and the Waiting Game
Captain David Alaba returns from the knee injury that ruled him out of Euro 24 — he travelled to Germany as a non-playing skipper and watched from the stands. At 33, this is finally his first World Cup. He has won the Champions League four times — twice with Bayern Munich, twice with Real Madrid — and brings that winning mentality to a squad that has never experienced a tournament at this level. Marko Arnautović, 37, is Austria's all-time top scorer with 47 goals in 132 caps — the veteran finally reaching the World Cup at the tail end of an extraordinary career. Marcel Sabitzer drives the midfield from Borussia Dortmund and is set to reach 100 caps at this tournament. Christoph Baumgartner arrives in the form of his life having scored 13 goals in his best Bundesliga season yet.

Group J: My Take
Argentina top this group. The real fight is between Austria and Algeria for second place — and I genuinely cannot split them. Both are capable sides. Both have a point to prove. Both know that the head-to-head on June 28 likely decides everything. Austria's qualifying form under Rangnick was dominant — six wins, one draw, just four goals conceded in eight matches. That defensive organization is going to matter. Jordan are the must-win opener to set the tone.
My prediction: Argentina first. Austria or Algeria second — the June 28 clash decides it. Austria have the squad to go deep if they get through. Under Rangnick, don't bet against them.



Jordan Team page

Jordan at the 2026 World Cup
Jordan — Al Nashama, The Chivalrous Ones — are at the World Cup for the first time in their history. After decades of near misses and heartbreak in Asian qualification, the moment finally arrived when a 3-0 victory over Oman, combined with Iraq's loss, sealed their historic ticket to North America. It follows a remarkable run of form that included reaching the AFC Asian Cup final in 2024 and the 2025 Arab Cup final — Jordan have been building quietly and consistently, and the World Cup is the reward. 

The Coach Who Dares to Dream
Coach Jamal Sellami — who played for Morocco at the 1998 World Cup — has openly urged his players to emulate the greatest upsets in World Cup history: Algeria beating West Germany in 1982, Cameroon stunning Argentina in 1990, Senegal beating France in 2002. That is the standard he is pointing at. "These results open horizons of hope and ambition for the fans, so they can dream. And we too have the right to dream." A coach who arrived from Morocco's domestic scene, won the CAF Confederation Cup with Raja Casablanca, and now leads a nation to their first ever World Cup. He has already achieved the impossible once. 

The Players to Watch
Mousa Al-Tamari — nicknamed the Jordanian Messi — is the best player Jordan have ever produced. The Rennes winger scored seven goals and provided 11 assists in 36 club appearances last season, then added seven more in Asian qualifying. Everything Jordan do going forward is built around releasing him into space on the counter. Ali Olwan returns from injury as Jordan's all-time top scorer with 29 international goals — his hat-trick sealed qualification and his partnership with Al-Tamari gives Jordan a genuine attacking threat against anyone. The tactical system is a compact 3-4-2-1 that becomes a 5-4-1 out of possession — designed to frustrate stronger sides and hurt them on the break. It is exactly the kind of disciplined, counter-attacking football that can cause problems at tournament level. 

Group J and the Biggest Match in Jordan's History
Group J — Argentina, Algeria, Austria, Jordan — hands Jordan the toughest possible debut. The opener against Austria in Santa Clara is their most winnable game — the result that defines everything. Algeria next, then the final group game against reigning champions Argentina in Dallas. Jordan vs Argentina could be the biggest match in the Arab nation's history — Messi on one side, Al-Tamari on the other. Whatever happens in that group, Jordan have already made history just by being there. Al Nashama — The Chivalrous Ones — have earned their moment on the world stage.

DR Congo Team page

DR Congo at the 2026 World Cup
The first sub-Saharan African nation ever to qualify for a World Cup — back in 1974 as Zaire — DR Congo returned as the 47th team to complete the 2026 roster, sealing their spot just hours before Iraq became the final 48th. Axel Tuanzebe's 100th-minute winner against Jamaica at Estadio Akron on March 31 sealed the dream — and sparked scenes of celebration across Kinshasa that 52 years of waiting had been building toward. The Leopards are back.

52 Years and the Road to Get Here
DR Congo's only previous World Cup appearance came in 1974, when they played three group stage games, lost all three, and conceded 14 goals without scoring once. It was a brutal introduction to the world stage. Half a century later, this squad arrives with genuine quality and a qualification campaign that demanded they beat the best Africa had to offer. They beat Cameroon 1-0 in the CAF playoff semi-final, then defeated Nigeria in the final — drawing 1-1 before winning 4-3 on penalties. Cameroon and Nigeria. Both eliminated by the Leopards. Then Jamaica beaten in extra time to seal the final spot. This was not a lucky qualification. This was earned the hard way.

The Squad
Captain Chancel Mbemba marshals the defense with 107 caps — the Lille center-back has rebuilt his reputation since difficult days at Newcastle and now leads his country to their greatest moment. Yoane Wissa of Newcastle leads the line as the squad's most dangerous attacker — pace, movement, and Premier League-proven quality. Aaron Wan-Bissaka switched allegiance from England to the Leopards and gives DR Congo Premier League quality at right back. Cédric Bakambu at 35 sits just one goal short of DR Congo's all-time scoring record — could he break it on the biggest stage? Noah Sadiki — box-to-box midfielder, explosive ball-carrier, tactical maturity beyond his years — told reporters that "this qualification must be the beginning of getting our great country at every World Cup going forward." That ambition runs through the entire squad.

Group K and the Dream
Group K — Portugal, Colombia, Uzbekistan, DR Congo — is a tough welcome back to the world stage. Portugal are one of the tournament favorites. Colombia are a genuine dark horse. But the Uzbekistan game in Atlanta on June 28 is a very winnable match — and a win there combined with a competitive showing against Colombia could be enough to sneak through as one of the best third-place finishers. Every single player in Desabre's 26-man squad is a World Cup first-timer. An entire nation making their debut together. The romance of that is undeniable — and this squad has already shown they can beat anyone on their day.
They beat Cameroon. They beat Nigeria. They beat Jamaica in extra time. Don't put anything past the Leopards.
