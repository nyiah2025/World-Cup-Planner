Senegal Team page

Senegal at the 2026 World Cup
In their very first World Cup match in 2002, Senegal shocked the world by beating defending champions France 1-0 — then topped a group containing Denmark and Uruguay, knocked out Sweden in the last 16, and only narrowly fell to Türkiye in the quarterfinals. Nearly a quarter of a century later, the Lions of Teranga arrive at 2026 as arguably Africa's best side, with a squad deeper and more experienced than any they have sent to a World Cup before. That 2002 magic is the benchmark. This squad believes it can go further. 

The AFCON Controversy
The squad announcement landed in the long shadow of one of the most dramatic incidents in recent African football history. Senegal beat Morocco in the AFCON 2025 final — then watched CAF's Appeal Board strip them of the title in March following a controversial last-minute penalty decision. It was a chaotic, painful episode that left Senegal furious and motivated in equal measure. They arrive at the World Cup with something to prove — not just on the pitch, but to the entire continent. 
Senegal became the first African side ever to beat England at Wembley, winning 3-1 in a friendly last June. That result sent a clear message about where this squad stands on the world stage. They are not here to be respectful opposition. They are here to compete. 

The Squad
Sadio Mané leads the line at 34 — Senegal's all-time record scorer with 55 international goals, still the face and inspiration of everything they do. Nicolas Jackson of Chelsea, Ismaïla Sarr of Crystal Palace and Iliman Ndiaye of Everton complete an attacking quartet rich in Premier League pedigree. Captain Kalidou Koulibaly anchors the defense with 103 caps, and Idrissa Gana Gueye — Senegal's most capped player ever at 131 appearances — screens in midfield. This is a squad built for a tournament run. 

Group I: My Take
Group I — France, Senegal, Norway, Iraq — is the group of death and Senegal drew the toughest possible path. The opener against France at MetLife Stadium on June 16 is one of the most anticipated matches of the entire group stage — a rematch of that famous 2002 upset. Norway with Haaland are dangerous. Iraq are the most beatable side. Second place in this group is genuinely hard to call between Senegal and Norway — whoever wins their head-to-head match on June 22 likely goes through.
I have Senegal second behind France — but this is the group where an upset could happen at any moment. The Lions of Teranga have been waiting 24 years to recapture 2002. This squad might just be good enough to do it.



Iraq Team page

Iraq were the 48th and final team to qualify for this tournament — the very last nation confirmed for the field. They sealed their place on March 31, 2026, beating Bolivia 2-1 in an intercontinental playoff in Monterrey, ending the longest absence of any team at the 2026 World Cup. Forty years. One previous appearance. One goal ever scored at a World Cup. The Lions of Mesopotamia are back — and they arrive as the ultimate underdogs with nothing to lose and everything to prove. 

The 40-Year Wait
Iraq's only previous World Cup came in 1986 in Mexico — where they lost all three group games, scored one goal, and registered just one shot on target across the entire tournament. That single goal — Ahmed Radhi's penalty against Belgium — remains an iconic national football moment to this day. An entire generation of Iraqi football has grown up without a World Cup to call their own. That changes in 2026, and the significance of simply being there cannot be overstated. 

The Road to Get Here
Iraq took the hard road. They finished third in their Asian third-round group behind South Korea and Jordan, which sent them into the playoff pathway rather than automatic qualification. The fourth round came down to the wire — Iraq beat Indonesia, drew with Saudi Arabia, then beat UAE across two legs to reach the intercontinental playoff. Then came Bolivia in Monterrey — and Aymen Hussein's winner sealed the deal. Twenty-one matches across 28 months of increasingly difficult competition. They earned every single minute of this.

The Squad
Aymen Hussein was Iraq's top scorer in qualifying with eight goals — the 30-year-old striker is the talisman and the player the entire squad rallies around. Nine players are based in Europe, from Zidane Iqbal at Utrecht to Ali Al-Hamadi at Ipswich Town — a diaspora core giving Iraq genuine technical quality across the squad. Captain Jalal Hassan leads the side with 100 caps — the experienced backbone this team needs in the biggest moments. 

Group I and the Dream
Group I — France, Senegal, Norway, Iraq — is as tough a draw as any team in the tournament received. The Norway opener is the realistic target for points — a game Iraq can approach with genuine belief. France and Senegal will be tough tests. But a team that survived 21 qualifying matches across two years, came through play-off after play-off, and sealed their place as the last team in the draw — that team does not come to North America just to make up the numbers. 
Iraq have one World Cup goal to their name from 1986. Aymen Hussein and this squad intend to change that story starting in June.


Norway Team page


Norway at the 2026 World Cup
Norway return to the World Cup after a 28-year absence as one of Europe's most in-form sides, buoyed by a golden generation that has been building quietly for years. The last time they were here was France 1998 — eliminated by Italy in the last 16. The road back to the World Cup took them through that same opponent. Norway beat Italy 3-0 in Oslo and 4-1 at the San Siro across their qualifying campaign — a statement so emphatic it left no doubt that this generation was ready for the biggest stage. 

The Father and Son Story
Alfie Haaland played for Norway at the 1994 World Cup. Now his son Erling takes the field at his first World Cup in 2026. That full circle moment is one of the great human stories of this tournament. Erling Haaland at 25 is already Norway's all-time top scorer with 55 international goals — in qualifying alone he scored 16 goals in eight matches, twice any other player in Europe. A generation of Norwegian fans who watched his father play in 1994 now watch his son arrive at the tournament as one of the most feared strikers on the planet. Manchester City's record-breaker. Norway's talisman. Finally at a World Cup. 

The Squad
Captain Martin Ødegaard drove Arsenal to their first Premier League title in 22 years — his creativity and vision is the supply line that makes Haaland so devastating. The one concern is fitness — Ødegaard suffered multiple injuries this season and missed Norway's March friendlies. A fully sharp Ødegaard is the difference between a group-stage exit and a genuine knockout-round run. Behind the headline duo, Alexander Sørloth of Atlético Madrid, Antonio Nusa of RB Leipzig and Jørgen Strand Larsen of Crystal Palace offer further attacking firepower — Norway have a depth of attacking quality that most teams in this tournament cannot match. 

Group I: My Take
Group I — France, Senegal, Norway, Iraq — is the toughest group in the draw and Norway drew it. They scored 37 goals and conceded just five across eight perfect qualifying matches — that form demands respect. The Iraq opener is the must-win. Then comes Senegal — the head-to-head that likely decides who joins France in the knockout rounds. I have Norway second in this group, but it is genuinely close between them and Senegal. If Ødegaard is fit and Haaland is on song, Norway are a team that can go very deep in this tournament.
My prediction: France first, Norway second — but the Norway vs Senegal game on June 23 at MetLife Stadium is one of the matches of the entire group stage. Don't miss it.


Algeria Team page


Algeria at the 2026 World Cup
In 1982, Algeria shocked defending European champions West Germany and nearly rewrote World Cup history — only to be eliminated by a scandalous gentlemen's agreement between West Germany and Austria that FIFA later banned. It's the reason the final group stage games are now played simultaneously. Forty-four years on, the Desert Foxes are back. And the draw handed them one intriguing subplot — Group J contains Austria, one of the two nations involved in that 1982 conspiracy. The wound never fully healed. The final group game between Algeria and Austria in Kansas City on June 28 carries history that goes well beyond three points.

Back After 12 Years
After a 12-year absence, Algeria return to the World Cup for the fifth time. Their best moment came in 2014 when they finally broke through the group stage for the first time, falling to eventual champions Germany in a thrilling extra-time battle in the Round of 16. That match still stings. This squad arrives with the same hunger to go further, under coach Vladimir Petković who previously guided Switzerland to the Euro 2020 quarterfinals.

Mahrez, Maza and the Next Generation
Riyad Mahrez, 35, heads to what he has called his last World Cup. The former Leicester and Manchester City star — Champions League winner, Premier League champion, AFCON winner — is Algeria's creative soul and the player their entire attacking game flows through. Behind him, Bayer Leverkusen's Ibrahim Maza has announced himself as one of Europe's most exciting young midfielders — the future of Algerian football arriving just as the present generation takes its final bow. Mohamed Amoura of Wolfsburg top-scored in CAF qualifying with 10 goals — the finishing edge that could genuinely unsettle Argentina. Then there is the wildcard — Luca Zidane, son of French legend Zinedine, recovered from a double chin and jaw fracture to make the squad as goalkeeper. Football writes the strangest stories.

Group J: My Take
Argentina top this group — that much is clear. The real battle is for second place between Algeria and Austria, and I genuinely cannot split them. The head-to-head in Kansas City on June 28 is the match that decides it — and given the 1982 history between Algeria and Austria, that game carries weight that goes well beyond three points.
My prediction: Argentina first. Algeria or Austria second — whoever wins their head-to-head goes through. That June 28 clash is one of the most historically loaded matches of the entire group stage.
