France team page

France at the 2026 World Cup
France arrive at 2026 as the world's number one ranked team and the 2022 runners-up, beaten in one of the greatest World Cup finals ever played when Argentina edged them on penalties after a 3-3 draw. Two years of hurt and hunger later, Didier Deschamps has a squad that many consider the most complete in the tournament. The mission is simple: finish the job.
The Mbappé Record Watch
Kylian Mbappé goes into this tournament with 12 World Cup goals from just two appearances — level with Pelé in the all-time list. Miroslav Klose's all-time record of 16 goals has stood since 2014, but it has never looked this vulnerable. Mbappé needs 4 goals to equal it and 5 to break it outright. At 27 years old, in the prime of his career, and with France expected to go deep into this tournament, that is very much on. Interestingly, Messi is also in the hunt at 13 goals — so the all-time record could fall to either of them this summer. Klose himself has already admitted the record won't last much longer.
The Group of Death
France landed in the toughest group of the tournament. Group I — France, Senegal, Norway, and the intercontinental playoff winner — is no walk in the park for the world's top ranked side. Senegal are African champions, physical, organized, and capable of beating anyone on their day. Norway, led by Erling Haaland making his World Cup debut, are dangerous going forward and will fancy their chances against anyone. This is the group stage test France did not want.
Group I: My Take
France should get through — their squad depth is too good not to. But this group will test them in a way the other top seeds won't face until the knockout rounds. I'd take France to top the group, but second place is genuinely hard to call. Senegal or Norway — I honestly can't split them. Whoever wins their head-to-head match probably goes through. That game alone is worth watching.
My prediction: France first, with Senegal and Norway fighting it out for second. All three will probably end up in the Round of 32.

Spain team page

Spain at the 2026 World Cup
The reigning European champions arrive at 2026 as one of the genuine favourites to win the whole thing. Spain last won the World Cup in 2010 — and since then their tournament record has been frankly disappointing, with group stage exits and early knockouts failing to match the standard of that golden generation. But this squad feels different. Under Luis de la Fuente, Spain have built a team that is arguably younger, faster, and more direct than the tiki-taka era — and the results back it up. They go into this tournament on a 28-game unbeaten run in normal time.

The Players to Watch
Two names define this Spain squad. Lamine Yamal, still only 18, is the most exciting young player in world football right now. He announced himself at Euro 2024 as a 16-year-old and has only gotten better — 24 goals and 18 assists across all competitions last season for Barcelona. There is a hamstring concern heading into the tournament but Spain expect him to be fit for the opener. Then there is Rodri, the Manchester City midfielder and 2024 Ballon d'Or winner — arguably the finest defensive midfielder on the planet and the tactical heart that allows Yamal and the rest to play freely.

Group H: My Take
Spain top this group — that much is straightforward. The interesting story is Uruguay. Under Marcelo Bielsa they have quietly rebuilt into a dangerous, organized side and I think they are the real dark horse of this tournament. Saudi Arabia shocked Argentina at Qatar 2022 which is a reminder never to sleep on them entirely, and Cape Verde are making their World Cup debut. But for second place, Uruguay are my pick comfortably.
My prediction: Spain first, Uruguay second — and both capable of going deep into the knockout rounds.

England Team page

England at the 2026 World Cup
Sixty years. That's how long England have been waiting since their only World Cup title in 1966. Every tournament since has come with hope, heartbreak, and the growing weight of expectation that follows the Three Lions everywhere. Under new manager Thomas Tuchel — who replaced Gareth Southgate after Euro 2024 — England arrive at 2026 with genuine quality in the squad. Declan Rice, Jude Bellingham and Bukayo Saka will all be at their second World Cup, with Harry Kane captaining the side and carrying the hopes of a nation once again.

The Opener Nobody in England Wanted
England kick off their campaign with their toughest Group L assignment — Croatia, ranked 11th in the world, in a repeat of the 2018 semifinal where Croatia won 2-1. That result still stings. England will be desperate to set the record straight immediately. After Croatia, Ghana follow on June 23 and Panama close out the group stage on June 27 — Ghana's first ever competitive meeting with England. 

Group L: My Take
Honestly? England are one of those teams I find hard to call. They have the players to go all the way and the squad depth to match anyone. But they also have a long history of making tournaments harder than they need to be. I think they top this group — Croatia are the only real test here, and Panama and Ghana shouldn't trouble them. Croatia second is my call, though Ghana with Mohammed Kudus and Antoine Semenyo in attack are more dangerous than their seeding suggests.
My prediction: England first, Croatia second. As for how far England go after the group stage — I genuinely have no idea, and I suspect neither does anyone else.


Portugal Team page

Portugal at the 2026 World Cup
Portugal arrive at 2026 as reigning Nations League champions and one of the genuine contenders — not just a famous name, but a team built to finally win it. Ranked fifth in the world under Roberto Martínez, this squad blends elite experience with players at their peak. Bruno Fernandes, Bernardo Silva, Vitinha, and Rafael Leão give Portugal a depth of quality that goes well beyond one man. But as always, one man dominates the conversation.

Ronaldo's Sixth
At 41, Cristiano Ronaldo is heading to a sixth World Cup — a record no man has reached — chasing the one major trophy that has always escaped him. He shares the record for most World Cup appearances with Messi, but at 41 he is doing it at an age nobody else has attempted at this level. For Portugal alone he has scored 143 international goals — the most in men's football history. Whether he is the starter or the impact substitute, this is almost certainly his final chapter on the biggest stage. The squad also carries a poignant note — a symbolic place for the late Diogo Jota, the Liverpool forward who passed away tragically earlier this year. Portugal will carry his memory into every game. 

Group K: My Take
Group K is Portugal, Colombia, DR Congo, and Uzbekistan. Portugal top this group — that's the easy part. The interesting battle is for second, and Colombia are my pick as the dark horse of the entire tournament. Luis Díaz spearheads Colombia after a double-winning debut season at Bayern Munich, and James Rodríguez — who dazzled the world at Brazil 2014 — is back in the squad adding experience and creativity. DR Congo are making their first World Cup appearance since 1974 and could surprise someone. Uzbekistan are the clear weakest side.
My prediction: Portugal first, Colombia second — and Colombia are a team I'll be watching closely in the knockouts.

Netherlands team page

Netherlands at the 2026 World Cup
There is no more heartbreaking story in World Cup history than the Netherlands. Three World Cup finals — 1974, 1978, and 2010 — and not a single title to show for it. Three times they have been the best team in the tournament. Three times they have gone home empty-handed. Under Ronald Koeman, with a squad built around Virgil van Dijk, Cody Gakpo, Ryan Gravenberch, and Tijjani Reijnders, this could finally be the year. Nobody is saying it loudly. Maybe that's exactly why it could happen. 

A Squad Built for This
This Netherlands side is not the flashy, "Clockwork Orange" total football era — but it is organised, deep, and dangerous. Van Dijk leads a defence that also features Micky van de Ven, Jurrien Timber and Jan Paul van Hecke. Reijnders is the engine in midfield — when he plays well, the whole team plays well. The one blow is the absence of Xavi Simons, who ruptured his ACL in April 2026 while playing for Tottenham — a real loss for a team that needed his creativity. But the depth is there to absorb it.

Group F: My Take
I have genuine high hopes for the Netherlands this tournament. Group F is Netherlands, Japan, Sweden, and Tunisia. Netherlands should get through — but Japan are the wildcard here. They are more than capable of an upset and if they beat the Dutch in the opener on June 14, the whole group gets complicated. Sweden and Tunisia are manageable, but Japan could genuinely win this group if they hit their stride early.
My prediction: Netherlands first, Japan second — but watch the opener closely. If Japan win that, flip those around.
